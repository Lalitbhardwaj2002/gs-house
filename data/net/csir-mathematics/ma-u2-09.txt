# Permutation groups, normal subgroups and homomorphisms

> Permutations give the most concrete groups, and by Cayley's theorem every group is one of them. Normal subgroups are the kernels of homomorphisms; counting homomorphisms and automorphisms is a favourite examination exercise.

## Permutation groups

- Every permutation is a product of **disjoint cycles**, uniquely up to order. Its order is the lcm of the cycle lengths.
- A cycle of length $k$ is a product of $k - 1$ transpositions; a permutation is **even** or **odd** according to the number of transpositions. A cycle of odd length is even.
- $A_n$, the even permutations, is a normal subgroup of index 2 in $S_n$. $S_n$ is generated by transpositions, and also by $(1\,2)$ and $(1\,2\,\dots\,n)$; $A_n$ is generated by 3-cycles.
- Two permutations are **conjugate in $S_n$** if and only if they have the same cycle type. So the number of conjugacy classes of $S_n$ is $p(n)$, the number of partitions of $n$.
- The number of permutations with $c_k$ cycles of length $k$ (cycle type) is $\frac{n!}{\prod_k k^{c_k}\, c_k!}$. The number of $k$-cycles in $S_n$ is $\frac{n!}{k\,(n-k)!}$.
- For $n \ge 2$, a conjugacy class of $S_n$ inside $A_n$ splits into two classes of $A_n$ of equal size exactly when its cycle lengths (fixed points included) are distinct odd numbers; otherwise it stays one class. In $A_5$ the 5-cycles split into two classes of 12.
- $Z(S_n)$ is trivial for $n \ge 3$. $A_n$ is **simple** for $n \ge 5$; $A_4$ is not simple (it contains the normal Klein four-group).

## Normal subgroups and quotient groups

- $H$ is **normal** in $G$ ($H \trianglelefteq G$) if $gHg^{-1} = H$ for all $g$, that is, if left and right cosets agree. Then the cosets form the **quotient group** $G/H$ of order $[G : H]$.
- Every subgroup of an abelian group is normal. A subgroup of **index 2** is normal. The centre, and the kernel of any homomorphism, are normal.
- If $H$ is the only subgroup of its order, it is normal. If $p$ is the smallest prime dividing $\lvert G \rvert$, a subgroup of index $p$ is normal.
- Normality is not transitive: in $D_4$, a subgroup of order 2 generated by a reflection is normal in a Klein four-subgroup, which is normal in $D_4$, but it is not normal in $D_4$.
- $G$ is **simple** if it has no normal subgroup other than $\{e\}$ and $G$. The abelian simple groups are those of prime order.
- The **commutator subgroup** $G'$ is generated by all $aba^{-1}b^{-1}$. $G/G'$ is abelian, and $G/N$ is abelian if and only if $G' \subseteq N$. $S_n' = A_n$.

## Homomorphisms

- $\varphi : G \to G_1$ is a **homomorphism** if $\varphi(ab) = \varphi(a)\varphi(b)$. Then $\varphi(e) = e$, $\varphi(a^{-1}) = \varphi(a)^{-1}$, and $o(\varphi(a))$ divides $o(a)$ when $o(a)$ is finite.
- $\varphi$ is one-one if and only if $\ker \varphi = \{e\}$.

! remember | Isomorphism theorems
- **First**: $G / \ker \varphi \cong \varphi(G)$. So for finite groups $\lvert \varphi(G) \rvert$ divides both $\lvert G \rvert$ and $\lvert G_1 \rvert$.
- **Second**: $HN/N \cong H/(H \cap N)$ for a subgroup $H$ and a normal subgroup $N$.
- **Third**: $(G/N)/(H/N) \cong G/H$ for normal subgroups $N \subseteq H$.
- **Correspondence**: the subgroups of $G/N$ correspond to the subgroups of $G$ containing $N$.
- **Cayley's theorem**: every group of order $n$ is isomorphic to a subgroup of $S_n$.

## Counting homomorphisms and automorphisms

| Maps | Number |
| Homomorphisms $\mathbb{Z}_m \to \mathbb{Z}_n$ | $\gcd(m, n)$ |
| Homomorphisms $\mathbb{Z} \to G$ | $\lvert G \rvert$ (one for each element) |
| Onto homomorphisms $\mathbb{Z}_m \to \mathbb{Z}_n$ | $\varphi(n)$ if $n \mid m$, otherwise 0 |
| One-one homomorphisms $\mathbb{Z}_m \to \mathbb{Z}_n$ | $\varphi(m)$ if $m \mid n$, otherwise 0 |
| Homomorphisms $\mathbb{Z}_n \to G$ | Number of $g \in G$ with $g^n = e$ |
| Homomorphisms $S_3 \to \mathbb{Z}_6$ | 2 (the image has order 1 or 2) |
| Homomorphisms $\mathbb{Q} \to \mathbb{Z}$ | 1 (only the trivial one) |

- **Automorphisms**: $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}_n) \cong U(n)$, of order $\varphi(n)$; $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}_2$; $\operatorname{Aut}(V_4) \cong S_3$; $\operatorname{Aut}(S_3) \cong S_3$; $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}_p^n) \cong GL_n(F_p)$.
- **Inner automorphisms** $x \mapsto gxg^{-1}$ form $\operatorname{Inn}(G) \cong G/Z(G)$, a normal subgroup of $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$. For abelian $G$ the only inner automorphism is the identity.
- $\operatorname{Aut}(S_n) \cong S_n$ for $n \ne 2, 6$.

## Direct products and finite abelian groups

- If $H, K$ are normal, $H \cap K = \{e\}$ and $HK = G$, then $G \cong H \times K$.
- **Fundamental theorem**: every finite abelian group is a direct product of cyclic groups of prime-power order, uniquely up to order.
- The number of abelian groups of order $p_1^{a_1} \cdots p_k^{a_k}$ is $p(a_1) \cdots p(a_k)$. Examples: order 8 gives 3, order 16 gives 5, order 36 gives 4, order 72 gives 6.
- A finite abelian group has a subgroup of every order dividing its order.

? When are two permutations conjugate in $S_n$? :: Exactly when they have the same cycle type.
? How many homomorphisms from $\mathbb{Z}_{12}$ to $\mathbb{Z}_{18}$? :: $\gcd(12, 18) = 6$.
? What is $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}_n)$? :: $U(n)$, of order $\varphi(n)$.
? How many abelian groups of order 72? :: $p(3)\,p(2) = 3 \times 2 = 6$.

! recap | One-minute revision
- Order of a permutation: lcm of cycle lengths. Conjugacy in $S_n$: cycle type.
- $G/\ker\varphi \cong \operatorname{Im}\varphi$; homomorphisms $\mathbb{Z}_m \to \mathbb{Z}_n$: $\gcd(m,n)$.
- $\operatorname{Inn}(G) \cong G/Z(G)$; abelian groups of order $p^k$: $p(k)$.
