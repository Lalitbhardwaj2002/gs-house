# Polynomial rings, fields and Galois theory

> Fields are built from polynomial rings: dividing $F[x]$ by an irreducible polynomial gives a larger field containing a root. Finite fields are completely known, and Galois theory matches the intermediate fields of an extension with the subgroups of a group.

## Polynomial rings and irreducibility

- For a field $F$, $F[x]$ is a Euclidean domain. A polynomial of degree $n$ has at most $n$ roots in $F$ (false over rings with zero divisors: $x^2 - 1$ has four roots in $\mathbb{Z}_8$).
- A polynomial of degree 2 or 3 over a field is irreducible if and only if it has no root in the field. For degree 4 or more, having no root is not enough.
- Over $\mathbb{C}$ the irreducible polynomials have degree 1; over $\mathbb{R}$ they have degree 1, or degree 2 with negative discriminant.

! remember | Irreducibility over $\mathbb{Q}$
- **Rational root test**: a rational root $\frac{r}{s}$ (in lowest terms) of $a_n x^n + \dots + a_0 \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$ has $r \mid a_0$ and $s \mid a_n$.
- **Gauss's lemma**: a primitive polynomial of positive degree in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ if and only if it is irreducible over $\mathbb{Z}$.
- **Eisenstein's criterion**: if a prime $p$ divides $a_0, \dots, a_{n-1}$, $p \nmid a_n$ and $p^2 \nmid a_0$, then the polynomial is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$.
- **Reduction modulo $p$**: if $p \nmid a_n$ and the reduction modulo $p$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Z}_p$, the polynomial is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$. The converse fails: $x^4 + 1$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ but reducible modulo every prime.
- The **cyclotomic polynomial** $\Phi_p(x) = x^{p-1} + \dots + x + 1$ is irreducible (apply Eisenstein to $\Phi_p(x + 1)$). In general $\Phi_n$ has degree $\varphi(n)$ and is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$.

## Field extensions

- If $K \supseteq F$, then $K$ is a vector space over $F$; its dimension is the **degree** $[K : F]$.
- **Tower law**: $[L : F] = [L : K][K : F]$.
- $\alpha$ is **algebraic** over $F$ if it is a root of a non-zero polynomial over $F$; otherwise **transcendental** ($\pi$, $e$ over $\mathbb{Q}$). Its **minimal polynomial** is irreducible, and $[F(\alpha) : F]$ is its degree.
- $F(\alpha) \cong F[x]/(m_\alpha(x))$. A finite extension is algebraic; the converse fails (the field of all algebraic numbers over $\mathbb{Q}$).
- The **splitting field** of $f$ over $F$ is the smallest field in which $f$ factors into linear factors; it is unique up to isomorphism and, for $f$ of degree $n$, has degree at most $n!$ over $F$.
- **Constructible numbers**: a real number constructible by ruler and compass has degree a power of 2 over $\mathbb{Q}$. So the cube cannot be doubled, the angle of $60^\circ$ cannot be trisected, and a regular 7-gon cannot be constructed.

| Extension | Degree |
| $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$ | 2 |
| $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2})$ | 3 (not normal) |
| $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{3}) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{3})$ | 4 |
| Splitting field of $x^3 - 2$ | 6 |
| Splitting field of $x^4 - 2$ | 8 |
| $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$, $\zeta_n$ a primitive $n$-th root of unity | $\varphi(n)$ |
| $\mathbb{C}$ over $\mathbb{R}$ | 2 |
| $\mathbb{R}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ | Infinite |

## Finite fields

- A finite field has $p^n$ elements for a prime $p$ (its characteristic) and some $n \ge 1$. For each prime power $q = p^n$ there is exactly one field $F_q$ up to isomorphism.
- $F_{p^n}$ is the splitting field of $x^{p^n} - x$ over $F_p$; its elements are exactly the roots.
- The multiplicative group $F_q^*$ is **cyclic** of order $q - 1$.
- $F_{p^m}$ is a subfield of $F_{p^n}$ if and only if $m \mid n$. So the number of subfields of $F_{p^n}$ is $\tau(n)$.
- $x^{p^n} - x$ is the product of all monic irreducible polynomials over $F_p$ whose degree divides $n$.
- The number of monic irreducible polynomials of degree $n$ over $F_q$ is $\frac{1}{n} \sum_{d \mid n} \mu(d)\, q^{n/d}$. Over $F_2$: 2, 1, 2, 3, 6 for degrees 1 to 5.
- $F_{p^n} \cong F_p[x]/(f)$ for any irreducible $f$ of degree $n$. $\mathbb{Z}_4$ is not a field; $F_4 = F_2[x]/(x^2 + x + 1)$.
- The **Frobenius map** $a \mapsto a^p$ is an automorphism of $F_{p^n}$. $\operatorname{Gal}(F_{p^n}/F_p)$ is cyclic of order $n$, generated by it.

## Galois theory

- An algebraic extension is **normal** if every irreducible polynomial over $F$ with one root in $K$ splits in $K$, and **separable** if minimal polynomials have no repeated roots. Algebraic extensions in characteristic 0 and of finite fields are separable.
- **Primitive element theorem**: a finite separable extension is simple, $K = F(\alpha)$. Example: $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{3}) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{3})$.
- $K/F$ is **Galois** if it is normal and separable. Then the **Galois group** $\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)$, the automorphisms of $K$ fixing $F$, has order $[K : F]$. In general $\lvert \operatorname{Aut}(K/F) \rvert \le [K : F]$.

! remember | Fundamental theorem of Galois theory
- For a finite Galois extension $K/F$ with group $G$, the subgroups $H$ of $G$ correspond one-to-one to the intermediate fields $E$, by $H \mapsto$ fixed field of $H$ and $E \mapsto \operatorname{Gal}(K/E)$. The correspondence reverses inclusions.
- $[K : E] = \lvert H \rvert$ and $[E : F] = [G : H]$.
- $E/F$ is normal (Galois) if and only if $H$ is a normal subgroup of $G$; then $\operatorname{Gal}(E/F) \cong G/H$.

| Extension | Galois group |
| $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})/\mathbb{Q}$ | $\mathbb{Z}_2$ |
| $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{3})/\mathbb{Q}$ | $\mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$ |
| Splitting field of $x^3 - 2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ | $S_3$ |
| Splitting field of $x^4 - 2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ | $D_4$ |
| $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)/\mathbb{Q}$ | $U(n)$ |
| $F_{p^n}/F_p$ | $\mathbb{Z}_n$ |
| $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2})/\mathbb{Q}$ | Not Galois; automorphism group trivial |

- An irreducible cubic over $\mathbb{Q}$ has Galois group $A_3$ if its discriminant is a square in $\mathbb{Q}$, and $S_3$ otherwise.
- Over a field of characteristic 0, a polynomial is **solvable by radicals** if and only if its Galois group is solvable. $S_n$ is not solvable for $n \ge 5$, so the general quintic is not solvable by radicals.
- An irreducible polynomial of prime degree $p$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ with exactly two non-real roots has Galois group $S_p$.

? How many elements can a finite field have? :: A prime power $p^n$, and there is exactly one field of each such size.
? When is $F_{p^m}$ a subfield of $F_{p^n}$? :: Exactly when $m$ divides $n$.
? Degree of the splitting field of $x^3 - 2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$? :: 6, with Galois group $S_3$.
? What is $\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)/\mathbb{Q})$? :: $U(n)$, of order $\varphi(n)$.
