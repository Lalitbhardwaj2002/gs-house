# Design of experiments

> A good design lets treatments be compared fairly and with small error. The three basic designs differ in how many sources of variation they remove; block designs are judged by connectedness, balance and orthogonality; factorial experiments study several factors together, with confounding used to keep blocks small.

## Principles and basic designs

- Fisher's three principles: **replication** (to estimate error), **randomisation** (to make the estimate valid) and **local control** or blocking (to reduce error).

| Design | Layout | Model | Error degrees of freedom |
| Completely randomised (CRD) | $v$ treatments allotted at random to $N$ units | $y_{ij} = \mu + \tau_i + \varepsilon_{ij}$ | $N - v$ |
| Randomised block (RBD) | $r$ blocks, each containing all $v$ treatments once | $y_{ij} = \mu + \tau_i + \beta_j + \varepsilon_{ij}$ | $(v - 1)(r - 1)$ |
| Latin square (LSD) | $v \times v$ square; each treatment once in every row and column | $y_{ijk} = \mu + \rho_i + \gamma_j + \tau_k + \varepsilon_{ijk}$ | $(v - 1)(v - 2)$ |

- **CRD**: any number of treatments and replications; suits homogeneous material; a missing observation causes no difficulty.
- **RBD**: removes one source of variation. Total $vr - 1 = (r - 1) + (v - 1) + (r - 1)(v - 1)$.
- **LSD**: removes two sources with only $v^2$ units, but needs rows, columns and treatments equal in number and no interactions among them. Total $v^2 - 1 = 3(v - 1) + (v - 1)(v - 2)$. A $2 \times 2$ square leaves no error degrees of freedom.
- **Missing plot** in an RBD: estimate $x = \frac{rB + vT - G}{(r - 1)(v - 1)}$, with $B$, $T$ the totals of the affected block and treatment and $G$ the grand total of the known values; the error loses one degree of freedom. In an LSD: $x = \frac{v(R + C + T) - 2G}{(v - 1)(v - 2)}$.
- **Latin squares**: there are 12 squares of order 3 (1 in standard form) and 576 of order 4 (4 in standard form). There are at most $v - 1$ mutually orthogonal Latin squares of order $v$, and exactly $v - 1$ when $v$ is a prime power. No pair of orthogonal squares exists for $v = 2$ or $v = 6$. Two orthogonal squares give a Graeco-Latin square.

## General block designs

- For $v$ treatments in $b$ blocks, the **incidence matrix** $N = (n_{ij})$ records how often treatment $i$ occurs in block $j$; $r_i$ are the replications and $k_j$ the block sizes.
- The **information matrix** is $C = R - NK^{-1}N'$, with $R = \operatorname{diag}(r_i)$ and $K = \operatorname{diag}(k_j)$. It is symmetric with zero row sums, so its rank is at most $v - 1$.

| Property | Meaning | Condition |
| Connected | Every treatment contrast is estimable | $\operatorname{rank} C = v - 1$ |
| Balanced (variance balanced) | All elementary contrasts $\tau_i - \tau_j$ are estimated with the same variance | $C = \theta\left(I - \frac{1}{v}J\right)$ with $\theta > 0$ |
| Orthogonal | Treatment and block effects are estimated independently | $n_{ij} = \frac{r_ik_j}{n}$ for all $i$, $j$, with $n = \sum r_i$ the number of plots |

- An RBD is connected, balanced and orthogonal. A BIBD is connected and balanced but not orthogonal.

## Balanced incomplete block designs

- A **BIBD** $(v, b, r, k, \lambda)$ has $v$ treatments in $b$ blocks of size $k < v$; each treatment occurs in $r$ blocks and each pair of treatments occurs together in $\lambda$ blocks.

! remember | Parameter relations
- $vr = bk$ and $\lambda(v - 1) = r(k - 1)$.
- **Fisher's inequality**: $b \ge v$, equivalently $r \ge k$.
- $NN' = (r - \lambda)I + \lambda J$, with $\det NN' = rk(r - \lambda)^{v-1}$.
- **Symmetric** design ($b = v$, $r = k$): any two blocks have exactly $\lambda$ treatments in common; if $v$ is even, $r - \lambda$ must be a perfect square.
- **Resolvable** design (blocks grouped into complete replications): $b \ge v + r - 1$.
- The **complement** of a BIBD is a BIBD with parameters $(v, b, b - r, v - k, b - 2r + \lambda)$.
- $C = \frac{\lambda v}{k}\left(I - \frac{1}{v}J\right)$; the variance of an estimated difference of two treatment effects is $\frac{2k\sigma^2}{\lambda v}$; the efficiency factor relative to an RBD is $\frac{\lambda v}{rk} < 1$.
- The relations are necessary but not sufficient: no design exists with $(22, 22, 7, 7, 2)$, since $v$ is even and $r - \lambda = 5$ is not a square.

| Design $(v, b, r, k, \lambda)$ | Remark |
| $(4, 6, 3, 2, 1)$ | All pairs of four treatments |
| $(7, 7, 3, 3, 1)$ | Symmetric; the Fano plane |
| $(9, 12, 4, 3, 1)$ | Resolvable |
| $\left(v, \binom{v}{k}, \binom{v-1}{k-1}, k, \binom{v-2}{k-2}\right)$ | Unreduced design: all subsets of size $k$ |

## Factorial experiments

- In a $2^k$ experiment $k$ factors each have two levels, giving $2^k$ treatment combinations. There are $2^k - 1$ effects, each with one degree of freedom: $k$ main effects, $\binom{k}{2}$ two-factor interactions, and so on.
- Every effect is a contrast of the treatment totals with coefficients $\pm 1$, and different effects are mutually orthogonal. With $r$ replicates, effect $= \frac{\text{contrast}}{r\,2^{k-1}}$ and its sum of squares $= \frac{\text{contrast}^2}{r\,2^k}$. **Yates' method** computes all the contrasts by repeated sums and differences.
- For a $2^2$ experiment with treatment combinations $(1), a, b, ab$: the contrast for $A$ is $ab + a - b - (1)$, for $B$ it is $ab + b - a - (1)$, and for $AB$ it is $ab + (1) - a - b$.
- Factorial experiments estimate interactions and use every observation for every effect; one-factor-at-a-time experiments do neither.

! remember | Confounding
- **Confounding** makes blocks smaller by mixing chosen (usually high order) interactions with block differences; those interactions can then not be separated from blocks.
- A $2^k$ experiment in $2^p$ blocks of size $2^{k-p}$ confounds $2^p - 1$ effects: $p$ chosen independently, and all their **generalised interactions** (products with squared letters dropped).
- Example: in a $2^4$ experiment in four blocks, confounding $ABC$ and $BCD$ also confounds $AD$.
- **Construction**: the principal block holds the treatment combinations that have an even number of letters in common with each confounded effect; the other blocks come by multiplying it by a combination not yet used. For $ABC$ in a $2^3$ experiment the blocks are $\{(1), ab, ac, bc\}$ and $\{a, b, c, abc\}$.
- **Complete confounding**: the same effects are confounded in every replicate, and all information on them is lost. **Partial confounding**: different effects in different replicates, so each is estimated from the replicates in which it is free.
- Main effects are never confounded by choice, and the generalised interactions must be checked too.

- A **fractional factorial** $2^{k-p}$ runs only a fraction of the combinations; effects are then **aliased** in sets given by the defining relation.

? Error degrees of freedom of a $v \times v$ Latin square? :: $(v - 1)(v - 2)$.
? The two parameter identities of a BIBD? :: $vr = bk$ and $\lambda(v - 1) = r(k - 1)$.
? What does Fisher's inequality say? :: $b \ge v$ for every BIBD.
? If $ABC$ and $BCD$ are confounded in a $2^4$ experiment, what else is confounded? :: Their generalised interaction $AD$.

! recap | One-minute revision
- Error degrees of freedom: CRD $N - v$; RBD $(v - 1)(r - 1)$; LSD $(v - 1)(v - 2)$.
- BIBD: $vr = bk$, $\lambda(v - 1) = r(k - 1)$, $b \ge v$; connected and balanced, not orthogonal.
- $2^k$ in $2^p$ blocks: $2^p - 1$ effects confounded, including generalised interactions.
