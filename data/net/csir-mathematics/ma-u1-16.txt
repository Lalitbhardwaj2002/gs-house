# Canonical forms: triangular and Jordan forms

> Not every matrix can be diagonalised, but over $\mathbb{C}$ every matrix is similar to a triangular matrix and, more precisely, to a Jordan form. Reading a Jordan form off the characteristic polynomial, the minimal polynomial and a few ranks is a standard examination task.

## Triangular form

- A matrix is **triangularisable** over $F$ if and only if its characteristic polynomial splits into linear factors over $F$. So every complex matrix is similar to an upper triangular matrix, with the eigenvalues on the diagonal.
- **Schur's theorem**: every complex square matrix is unitarily similar to an upper triangular matrix.
- A real matrix with a non-real eigenvalue is not triangularisable over $\mathbb{R}$: the rotation by $90^\circ$.

## Jordan blocks and the Jordan form

- The **Jordan block** $J_k(\lambda)$ is the $k \times k$ matrix with $\lambda$ on the diagonal, 1 on the superdiagonal and 0 elsewhere. It has one eigenvalue $\lambda$ with a single independent eigenvector, and $(J_k(\lambda) - \lambda I)^k = 0$ but $(J_k(\lambda) - \lambda I)^{k-1} \ne 0$.
- If the characteristic polynomial splits, $A$ is similar to a direct sum of Jordan blocks, unique up to the order of the blocks. Two such matrices are similar if and only if they have the same Jordan form.

! remember | Reading the Jordan form, for each eigenvalue $\lambda$
- The sum of the sizes of the blocks for $\lambda$ is the **algebraic multiplicity**.
- The number of blocks for $\lambda$ is the **geometric multiplicity** $n - \operatorname{rank}(A - \lambda I)$.
- The size of the largest block for $\lambda$ is the power of $(x - \lambda)$ in the **minimal polynomial**.
- The number of blocks of size at least $k$ is $\operatorname{rank}(A - \lambda I)^{k-1} - \operatorname{rank}(A - \lambda I)^k$.
- $A$ is diagonalisable if and only if every block has size 1.

## Worked patterns

| Given | Possible block sizes | How to decide |
| Characteristic $(x-2)^4$, minimal $(x-2)^2$ | $2,2$ or $2,1,1$ | GM is 2 or 3 |
| Characteristic $(x-2)^4$, minimal $(x-2)^3$ | $3,1$ | Only one possibility |
| Characteristic $(x-1)^3 (x-2)^2$, minimal $(x-1)^2 (x-2)$ | $2,1$ for $\lambda = 1$; $1,1$ for $\lambda = 2$ | Only one possibility |
| $6 \times 6$ nilpotent, $A^3 \ne 0$, $A^4 = 0$ | $4,2$ or $4,1,1$ | Largest block 4; 2 or 3 blocks |
| $A^2 = 0$, $A \ne 0$, order $n$ | Blocks of size 2 and 1 | Number of blocks of size 2 is $\operatorname{rank} A$ |

## Counting similarity classes

- The number of similarity classes of $n \times n$ **nilpotent** matrices is $p(n)$, the number of partitions of $n$: $p(1), \dots, p(7) = 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 15$.
- With characteristic polynomial $\prod (x - \lambda_i)^{a_i}$ (distinct $\lambda_i$), the number of possible Jordan forms is $\prod p(a_i)$.
- For a nilpotent matrix of index $k$ (largest block $k$) the possible forms are the partitions of $n$ whose largest part is $k$.
- If $f(A) = 0$ for a polynomial $f$ with $k$ distinct roots, all in the field, then $A$ is diagonalisable with eigenvalues among those roots, so there are $\binom{n+k-1}{k-1}$ similarity classes. Example: $3 \times 3$ complex matrices with $A^3 = I$ form $\binom{5}{2} = 10$ classes.

## Further facts

- Every square matrix is similar to its transpose.
- The nilpotency index of an $n \times n$ nilpotent matrix is at most $n$; it equals $n$ exactly for a single Jordan block, when the rank is $n - 1$.
- $A$ and $B$ with the same characteristic and minimal polynomials are similar when $n \le 3$; this fails for $n = 4$ (block sizes $2,2$ against $2,1,1$).
- **Rational canonical form**: over any field, a matrix is similar to a direct sum of companion matrices of its invariant factors, the last of which is the minimal polynomial. It needs no eigenvalues in the field.
- Jordan decomposition: over $\mathbb{C}$, $A = D + N$ with $D$ diagonalisable, $N$ nilpotent and $DN = ND$, uniquely.

? What does the number of Jordan blocks for $\lambda$ equal? :: The geometric multiplicity of $\lambda$.
? What does the largest block size for $\lambda$ equal? :: The exponent of $(x - \lambda)$ in the minimal polynomial.
? How many nilpotent $4 \times 4$ matrices are there up to similarity? :: $p(4) = 5$.
? Are matrices with equal characteristic and minimal polynomials always similar? :: Only up to order 3.

! exam | PYQ focus
- Possible Jordan forms from the characteristic and minimal polynomials and a rank.
- Number of Jordan blocks, or of non-zero entries, in the Jordan form of a given operator.
- Counting matrices up to similarity under conditions such as $A^k = 0$.

! recap | One-minute revision
- Blocks for $\lambda$: total size is AM, number is GM, largest size is the exponent in the minimal polynomial.
- Nilpotent classes of order $n$: partitions of $n$.
- Diagonalisable exactly when all blocks have size 1.
