# Quadratic forms

> A quadratic form is a homogeneous polynomial of degree 2. Each one is given by a symmetric matrix, can be reduced to a sum of squares, and is classified by three numbers: rank, index and signature.

## Quadratic forms and their matrices

- A real quadratic form in $x = (x_1, \dots, x_n)$ is $Q(x) = x^T A x = \sum_{i,j} a_{ij} x_i x_j$ with $A$ **symmetric**.
- The matrix has the coefficient of $x_i^2$ at position $(i,i)$ and **half** the coefficient of $x_i x_j$ at positions $(i,j)$ and $(j,i)$.
- Example: $Q = x^2 + 4xy + 3y^2$ has matrix with rows $(1, 2)$ and $(2, 3)$.
- If a form is written as $x^T B x$ with $B$ not symmetric, its matrix is $\frac{1}{2}(B + B^T)$; definiteness is decided by this symmetric part, not by the eigenvalues of $B$.
- A change of variables $x = Py$ with $P$ invertible gives $Q = y^T (P^T A P) y$. The matrices $A$ and $P^T A P$ are **congruent**. Congruence keeps rank and symmetry, but not eigenvalues or determinant (the sign of the determinant is kept).

## Reduction to a sum of squares

- **Orthogonal reduction** (principal axes theorem): with $P$ orthogonal and $P^T A P$ diagonal, $Q = \lambda_1 y_1^2 + \dots + \lambda_n y_n^2$, where $\lambda_i$ are the eigenvalues of $A$.
- **Lagrange's method**: complete squares one variable at a time.
- **Congruent operations**: apply the same elementary operation to rows and to columns until $A$ is diagonal.
- **Canonical form**: $y_1^2 + \dots + y_p^2 - y_{p+1}^2 - \dots - y_r^2$.

! remember | Rank, index and signature
- **Rank** $r$: the rank of $A$, the number of non-zero squares.
- **Index** $p$: the number of positive squares, equal to the number of positive eigenvalues of $A$.
- **Signature**: positive squares minus negative squares, $s = p - (r - p) = 2p - r$.
- **Sylvester's law of inertia**: $r$ and $p$ do not depend on the reduction used.
- Two real quadratic forms in $n$ variables are equivalent (their matrices congruent) if and only if they have the same rank and the same index.

## Classification

| Type | Values of $Q(x)$, $x \ne 0$ | Eigenvalues of $A$ | Rank and index |
| Positive definite | Always $> 0$ | All $> 0$ | $r = p = n$ |
| Positive semidefinite | Always $\ge 0$, sometimes 0 | All $\ge 0$, some 0 | $r = p < n$ |
| Negative definite | Always $< 0$ | All $< 0$ | $r = n$, $p = 0$ |
| Negative semidefinite | Always $\le 0$, sometimes 0 | All $\le 0$, some 0 | $r < n$, $p = 0$ |
| Indefinite | Both signs | Some $> 0$, some $< 0$ | $0 < p < r$ |

- **Sylvester's criterion**: $A$ is positive definite if and only if all leading principal minors $D_1, D_2, \dots, D_n$ are positive. It is negative definite if and only if $(-1)^k D_k > 0$ for every $k$.
- For positive **semi**definiteness all principal minors (not only the leading ones) must be non-negative.

! remember | Facts on positive definite matrices
- $A$ is positive definite if and only if $A = B^T B$ for some invertible $B$.
- Then $\det A > 0$, $\operatorname{tr} A > 0$, every diagonal entry is positive, and $A^{-1}$ is positive definite.
- The sum of two positive definite matrices is positive definite. The product need not even be symmetric, but its eigenvalues are positive; if the two commute, the product is positive definite.
- $A^T A$ is always positive semidefinite, and positive definite exactly when the columns of $A$ are linearly independent.
- $\langle x, y \rangle = x^T A y$ is an inner product on $\mathbb{R}^n$ exactly when $A$ is symmetric positive definite.

## Counting and extremes

- Real symmetric $n \times n$ matrices fall into $\frac{(n+1)(n+2)}{2}$ congruence classes (choices of $p$ and $r$ with $0 \le p \le r \le n$). Complex symmetric matrices under congruence are classified by rank alone: $n + 1$ classes.
- **Rayleigh quotient**: $\lambda_{\min} \le \frac{x^T A x}{x^T x} \le \lambda_{\max}$, so on the unit sphere $Q$ has maximum $\lambda_{\max}$ and minimum $\lambda_{\min}$.
- A **Hermitian form** $x^* A x$ with $A$ Hermitian takes real values and is classified in the same way.

? How is the cross term $x_i x_j$ entered in the matrix? :: Half its coefficient goes to each of the positions $(i,j)$ and $(j,i)$.
? Signature in terms of rank and index? :: $s = 2p - r$.
? Test for positive definiteness without eigenvalues? :: All leading principal minors are positive.
? Number of congruence classes of real symmetric $3 \times 3$ matrices? :: $\frac{4 \cdot 5}{2} = 10$.
