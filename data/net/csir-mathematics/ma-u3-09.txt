# Classical mechanics

> Lagrange's and Hamilton's methods describe a mechanical system by a single function, the Lagrangian $L = T - V$ or the Hamiltonian $H$, in coordinates chosen to fit the constraints. Rigid bodies and small oscillations are the two standard applications.

## Constraints and generalised coordinates

- A system of $N$ particles with $k$ independent holonomic constraints has $n = 3N - k$ **degrees of freedom**, described by generalised coordinates $q_1, \dots, q_n$.

| Constraint | Meaning | Example |
| Holonomic | An equation $f(\mathbf{r}_1, \dots, \mathbf{r}_N, t) = 0$ | Particle on a sphere; rigid body |
| Non-holonomic | An inequality, or a relation between velocities that cannot be integrated | Gas in a container; sphere rolling without slipping on a plane |
| Scleronomic | Does not contain time | Pendulum of fixed length |
| Rheonomic | Contains time explicitly | Bead on a rotating wire |

| System | Degrees of freedom |
| Free particle in space | 3 |
| Particle on a surface | 2 |
| Simple pendulum in a plane | 1 |
| Double pendulum in a plane | 2 |
| Two particles joined by a rigid rod | 5 |
| Free rigid body | 6 |
| Rigid body with one point fixed | 3 |
| Rigid body turning about a fixed axis | 1 |

- **D'Alembert's principle**: $\sum_i (\mathbf{F}_i - \dot{\mathbf{p}}_i)\cdot\delta\mathbf{r}_i = 0$, with $\mathbf{F}_i$ the applied forces, for virtual displacements consistent with the constraints; it assumes that the forces of constraint do no virtual work.

## Lagrange's equations

- For a holonomic system with potential $V$, $L = T - V$ and

$$\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{q}_j}\right) - \frac{\partial L}{\partial q_j} = 0, \qquad j = 1, \dots, n$$

- With forces not derived from a potential the right-hand side is the generalised force $Q_j$.
- The **generalised momentum** is $p_j = \frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{q}_j}$. If $L$ does not contain $q_j$ (a **cyclic** coordinate), $p_j$ is conserved.
- If $L$ does not contain $t$, the energy function $h = \sum_j \dot{q}_j p_j - L$ is conserved. It equals $T + V$ when the constraints are scleronomic and $V$ does not depend on the velocities.
- Symmetry gives conservation: invariance under translation, rotation and time shift gives conservation of linear momentum, angular momentum and energy.
- $L$ is not unique: adding $\frac{d}{dt}F(q, t)$ does not change the equations of motion.

| System | Lagrangian |
| Particle in a central field (plane polar coordinates) | $\frac{1}{2}m(\dot{r}^2 + r^2\dot{\theta}^2) - V(r)$ |
| Simple pendulum | $\frac{1}{2}ml^2\dot{\theta}^2 + mgl\cos\theta$ |
| Harmonic oscillator | $\frac{1}{2}m\dot{x}^2 - \frac{1}{2}kx^2$ |
| Spherical pendulum | $\frac{1}{2}ml^2(\dot{\theta}^2 + \sin^2\theta\,\dot{\varphi}^2) + mgl\cos\theta$ |
| Atwood's machine | $\frac{1}{2}(m_1 + m_2)\dot{x}^2 + (m_1 - m_2)gx$ |

- In a central field $\theta$ is cyclic, so $mr^2\dot{\theta}$ is constant: equal areas are swept in equal times.

## Hamilton's equations

- The **Hamiltonian** is $H(q, p, t) = \sum_j p_j\dot{q}_j - L$, with the velocities expressed through the momenta. The equations of motion are

$$\dot{q}_j = \frac{\partial H}{\partial p_j}, \qquad \dot{p}_j = -\frac{\partial H}{\partial q_j}$$

- These are $2n$ first order equations in place of $n$ second order ones. Also $\frac{dH}{dt} = \frac{\partial H}{\partial t} = -\frac{\partial L}{\partial t}$, so $H$ is conserved when it does not contain $t$.
- $H = T + V$ under the same conditions as for the energy function.
- Example: $H = \frac{p^2}{2m} + \frac{1}{2}kx^2$ gives $\dot{x} = \frac{p}{m}$, $\dot{p} = -kx$.

! remember | Poisson brackets and canonical transformations
- $\{F, G\} = \sum_j \left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial q_j}\frac{\partial G}{\partial p_j} - \frac{\partial F}{\partial p_j}\frac{\partial G}{\partial q_j}\right)$.
- $\{q_i, p_j\} = \delta_{ij}$, $\{q_i, q_j\} = 0$, $\{p_i, p_j\} = 0$.
- $\frac{dF}{dt} = \{F, H\} + \frac{\partial F}{\partial t}$. A function without explicit $t$ is a constant of the motion exactly when $\{F, H\} = 0$.
- The bracket is antisymmetric and satisfies the Jacobi identity; the bracket of two constants of the motion is again a constant of the motion (**Poisson's theorem**).
- A transformation $(q, p) \to (Q, P)$ is **canonical** if it keeps the form of Hamilton's equations. For one degree of freedom the test is $\{Q, P\} = 1$.
- Generating functions: $F_1(q, Q)$ gives $p = \frac{\partial F_1}{\partial q}$, $P = -\frac{\partial F_1}{\partial Q}$; $F_2(q, P)$ gives $p = \frac{\partial F_2}{\partial q}$, $Q = \frac{\partial F_2}{\partial P}$.
- **Liouville's theorem**: the flow in phase space preserves volume.

## Variational principles

- **Hamilton's principle**: for a holonomic system with forces derived from a potential, the actual motion between fixed positions at fixed times $t_1$, $t_2$ makes $\int_{t_1}^{t_2} L\,dt$ stationary. Its Euler-Lagrange equations are Lagrange's equations.
- **Principle of least action** (Maupertuis): for a scleronomic system with $H$ conserved, among paths with the same energy (the time of travel is not fixed) the actual path makes the action $\int \sum_j p_j\dot{q}_j\,dt = \int 2T\,dt$ stationary.
- **Jacobi's form**: for one particle, $\int \sqrt{2m(E - V)}\,ds$ is stationary, an integral over the path with time eliminated.

## Rigid bodies

| Body, mass $M$ | Axis | Moment of inertia |
| Thin rod, length $L$ | Through the centre, perpendicular | $\frac{ML^2}{12}$ |
| Thin rod, length $L$ | Through one end, perpendicular | $\frac{ML^2}{3}$ |
| Ring, radius $R$ | Axis of the ring | $MR^2$ |
| Disc, radius $R$ | Axis of the disc | $\frac{MR^2}{2}$ |
| Disc, radius $R$ | A diameter | $\frac{MR^2}{4}$ |
| Solid sphere | A diameter | $\frac{2MR^2}{5}$ |
| Thin spherical shell | A diameter | $\frac{2MR^2}{3}$ |

- **Parallel axes**: $I = I_{cm} + Md^2$. **Perpendicular axes** (plane lamina): $I_z = I_x + I_y$.
- **Motion in a plane**: the centre of mass moves as a particle of mass $M$ under the total force, and $I_{cm}\ddot{\theta}$ equals the torque about the centre of mass. $T = \frac{1}{2}Mv_{cm}^2 + \frac{1}{2}I_{cm}\omega^2$.
- **Rolling without slipping** down a plane of inclination $\alpha$: acceleration $\frac{g\sin\alpha}{1 + k^2/R^2}$, where $Mk^2$ is the moment of inertia about the axis through the centre. Solid sphere $\frac{5}{7}g\sin\alpha$, disc $\frac{2}{3}g\sin\alpha$, ring $\frac{1}{2}g\sin\alpha$.
- **Compound pendulum**: period $2\pi\sqrt{\frac{I}{Mgh}}$, with $I$ about the pivot and $h$ the distance of the centre of mass from it.

! remember | Euler's dynamical equations
- For a body turning about a fixed point, in principal axes fixed in the body:

$$I_1\dot{\omega}_1 - (I_2 - I_3)\omega_2\omega_3 = N_1, \quad I_2\dot{\omega}_2 - (I_3 - I_1)\omega_3\omega_1 = N_2, \quad I_3\dot{\omega}_3 - (I_1 - I_2)\omega_1\omega_2 = N_3$$

- With no torque, the kinetic energy $\frac{1}{2}\sum I_i\omega_i^2$ and the squared angular momentum $\sum I_i^2\omega_i^2$ are constant.
- Torque-free rotation about the principal axes of greatest and least moment is stable; about the intermediate axis it is unstable.
- Symmetric body ($I_1 = I_2$), no torque: $\omega_3$ is constant and the angular velocity vector turns about the axis of symmetry at the rate $\frac{I_3 - I_1}{I_1}\omega_3$.

## Small oscillations

- Near a position of stable equilibrium write $T = \frac{1}{2}\sum T_{ij}\dot{q}_i\dot{q}_j$ and $V = \frac{1}{2}\sum V_{ij}q_iq_j$, with $V_{ij}$ the second derivatives of $V$ at equilibrium. The **normal frequencies** are the roots of

$$\det(V - \omega^2 T) = 0$$

- A system with $n$ degrees of freedom has $n$ normal modes. All $\omega^2$ are real; they are all positive when the matrix $(V_{ij})$ is positive definite, and a zero root means a free translation or rotation.
- In **normal coordinates** the motion separates into independent harmonic oscillations.
- Equilibrium is stable when $V$ has a strict minimum there.

| System | $\omega^2$ of the normal modes |
| Two equal masses $m$ and three equal springs $k$ between walls | $\frac{k}{m}$, $\frac{3k}{m}$ |
| Two pendulums (length $l$, mass $m$) joined by a spring $k$ | $\frac{g}{l}$, $\frac{g}{l} + \frac{2k}{m}$ |
| Linear molecule $m$-$M$-$m$ with springs $k$ | $0$, $\frac{k}{m}$, $\frac{k}{m}\left(1 + \frac{2m}{M}\right)$ |
| Double pendulum, equal masses and lengths | $(2 \pm \sqrt{2})\frac{g}{l}$ |

? Degrees of freedom of a rigid body with one point fixed? :: 3.
? What is conserved when a coordinate is cyclic? :: Its conjugate momentum $p_j = \frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{q}_j}$.
? Hamilton's canonical equations? :: $\dot{q}_j = \frac{\partial H}{\partial p_j}$ and $\dot{p}_j = -\frac{\partial H}{\partial q_j}$.
? About which principal axis is torque-free rotation unstable? :: The axis of the intermediate moment of inertia.

- Lagrangian or Hamiltonian of a given system, its equations of motion and its conserved quantities.
- Poisson brackets; whether a given transformation is canonical.
- Normal frequencies of a coupled system; moments of inertia and plane motion of a rigid body.

! recap | One-minute revision
- $L = T - V$; cyclic coordinate gives conserved momentum; no explicit time gives conserved energy function.
- $H = \sum p\dot{q} - L$; $\dot{q} = \frac{\partial H}{\partial p}$, $\dot{p} = -\frac{\partial H}{\partial q}$; $\frac{dF}{dt} = \{F, H\} + \frac{\partial F}{\partial t}$.
- Normal modes: $\det(V - \omega^2 T) = 0$. Free rigid body: 6 degrees of freedom; Euler's equations in principal axes.
