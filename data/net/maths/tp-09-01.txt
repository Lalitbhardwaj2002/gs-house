# Trigonometric ratios and the unit circle

> Angles are measured in radians; on the unit circle the point at angle $\theta$ is $(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$. The signs in the four quadrants (ASTC), the allied-angle rules and the values at special angles answer most one-mark questions.

## Radians, the unit circle and the signs

^ data/net/maths/fig/unit-circle.svg | the unit circle: $P(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$, with the quadrants where All, Sin, Tan and Cos are positive

! note | Circular measure and the unit circle
**Circular measure**: $\pi$ radians $= 180°$; arc length $l = r\theta$; sector area $\frac{1}{2}r^2\theta$.
On the unit circle, the point at angle $\theta$ is $P(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$; $\tan\theta = \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}$.
**Signs (ASTC)**: Quadrant I all positive; II only $\sin$, $\operatorname{cosec}$; III only $\tan$, $\cot$; IV only $\cos$, $\sec$.
**Allied angles**: $\sin(\pi - \theta) = \sin\theta$, $\cos(\pi - \theta) = -\cos\theta$, $\sin(\pi + \theta) = -\sin\theta$, $\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - \theta\right) = \cos\theta$, $\sin(-\theta) = -\sin\theta$, $\cos(-\theta) = \cos\theta$.

! check | A condition added
$l = r\theta$ and the sector area $\frac{1}{2}r^2\theta$ need $\theta$ in **radians**; in degrees they read $l = \frac{\pi r\theta}{180}$ and $\frac{\pi r^2\theta}{360}$. $\tan\theta = \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}$ is defined only where $\cos\theta \neq 0$.

## Values at special angles

! note | Values of special angles
| $\theta$ | $0°$ | $30°$ | $45°$ | $60°$ | $90°$ |
| $\sin\theta$ | $0$ | $\frac{1}{2}$ | $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ | $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ | $1$ |
| $\cos\theta$ | $1$ | $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ | $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ | $\frac{1}{2}$ | $0$ |
| $\tan\theta$ | $0$ | $\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$ | $1$ | $\sqrt{3}$ | $\infty$ |
Also $\sin 18° = \frac{\sqrt{5} - 1}{4}$, $\cos 36° = \frac{\sqrt{5} + 1}{4}$, $\tan 15° = 2 - \sqrt{3}$, $\tan 75° = 2 + \sqrt{3}$, $\tan 22\frac{1}{2}° = \sqrt{2} - 1$.

! check | A condition added
"$\tan 90° = \infty$" is shorthand: $\tan\theta$ is **not defined** at $90°$; it grows without bound as $\theta \to 90°$ from below (and to $-\infty$ from above).

## What it means

- **Radian**: the angle that cuts off an arc equal to the radius. A full turn is $2\pi$ radians, so $1 \text{ rad} = \frac{180°}{\pi} \approx 57.3°$ and $1° = \frac{\pi}{180}$ rad.
- **Allied angles in one rule**: for $\frac{n\pi}{2} \pm \theta$, the function stays the same when $n$ is even and changes to its co-function ($\sin \leftrightarrow \cos$, $\tan \leftrightarrow \cot$, $\sec \leftrightarrow \operatorname{cosec}$) when $n$ is odd; the sign is the sign of the original function in the quadrant where $\frac{n\pi}{2} \pm \theta$ lies (take $\theta$ acute).
- **Periods**: $\sin$, $\cos$, $\sec$, $\operatorname{cosec}$ repeat every $2\pi$; $\tan$ and $\cot$ every $\pi$.
- **Ranges**: $-1 \leq \sin\theta, \cos\theta \leq 1$; $\lvert \sec\theta \rvert, \lvert \operatorname{cosec}\theta \rvert \geq 1$; $\tan$ and $\cot$ take every real value.

! example | Example 1 — degrees to radians
Express $40°20'$ in radians.
**Solution.** $40°20' = 40\frac{1}{3}° = \frac{121}{3}°$, and $\frac{121}{3} \cdot \frac{\pi}{180} = \boxed{\frac{121\pi}{540}}$ rad.

! example | Example 2 — allied angles
Find $\sin 120°$, $\cos 210°$ and $\tan(-300°)$.
**Solution.** $\sin 120° = \sin(180° - 60°) = \boxed{\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}$. $\cos 210° = \cos(180° + 30°) = \boxed{-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}$. And
$$\begin{aligned} &\tan(-300°) = -\tan 300° \\ &= -\tan(360° - 60°) \\ &= \tan 60° = \boxed{\sqrt{3}}. \end{aligned}$$

! example | Example 3 — arc and sector
A wheel of radius 35 cm turns through $72°$. How far does a point on its rim move? ($\pi = \frac{22}{7}$)
**Solution.** $\theta = \frac{72\pi}{180} = \frac{2\pi}{5}$; $l = r\theta = 35 \cdot \frac{2}{5} \cdot \frac{22}{7} = \boxed{44\text{ cm}}$.

? Which ratios are positive in the third quadrant? :: Only $\tan$ and $\cot$.
? $\sin 18°$ and $\cos 36°$? :: $\frac{\sqrt{5} - 1}{4}$ and $\frac{\sqrt{5} + 1}{4}$.
? Arc length and sector area for radius $r$ and angle $\theta$ radians? :: $l = r\theta$; area $\frac{1}{2}r^2\theta$.
? $\cos\left(\frac{3\pi}{2} + \theta\right)$? :: $\sin\theta$ (odd multiple: co-function; fourth quadrant: $\cos$ positive).

! exam | What the exam asks
- Degree–radian conversion, arc length, sector area.
- Signs and allied angles; values at $15°$, $18°$, $22\frac{1}{2}°$, $36°$.
- Periods and ranges of the six functions.

! recap | One-minute revision
- $\pi$ rad $= 180°$; $l = r\theta$; area $\frac{1}{2}r^2\theta$.
- ASTC; $\frac{n\pi}{2} \pm \theta$: co-function when $n$ is odd, sign by quadrant.
- $\tan 15° = 2 - \sqrt{3}$, $\tan 75° = 2 + \sqrt{3}$, $\tan 22\frac{1}{2}° = \sqrt{2} - 1$.
