# Volumes: all questions of the exercise, fully solved

> Every question below follows the same three steps: trace the curve (gallery), choose the strip perpendicular to the axis of rotation, and integrate $\pi(\text{radius})^2$ along the axis. Wallis' formula finishes most of the trigonometric integrals.

## Spheres, caps and paraboloids

! example | Question V1 — Hemisphere
Find the volume of a hemisphere of radius $a$.
**Solution.**
^ data/net/maths/fig/vol-hemisphere.svg | the quadrant of $x^2 + y^2 = a^2$ turning about the $x$-axis
The hemisphere is generated by revolving the quadrant of the circle $x^2 + y^2 = a^2$ (first quadrant) about the $x$-axis. Here $y^2 = a^2 - x^2$ and $x$ goes from 0 to $a$.
$$\begin{aligned} &V = \int_0^a \pi y^2\,dx \\ &= \pi\int_0^a (a^2 - x^2)\,dx \\ &= \pi\left[a^2x - \frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^a \\ &= \pi\left(a^3 - \frac{a^3}{3}\right) \\ &= \boxed{\frac{2}{3}\pi a^3} \end{aligned}$$

! example | Question V2 — Spherical cap
Find the volume of a spherical cap of height $h$ cut off from a sphere of radius $a$.
**Solution.**
^ data/net/maths/fig/vol-cap.svg | the cap between $x = a - h$ and $x = a$
The cap is generated by revolving the part of the circle $x^2 + y^2 = a^2$ between $x = a - h$ and $x = a$ about the $x$-axis.
$$\begin{aligned} &V = \pi\int_{a-h}^a (a^2 - x^2)\,dx \\ &= \pi\left[a^2x - \frac{x^3}{3}\right]_{a-h}^a \\ &= \pi\Big[\frac{2a^3}{3} - a^2(a - h) \\ &\qquad + \frac{(a - h)^3}{3}\Big]. \end{aligned}$$
Expanding $(a - h)^3 = a^3 - 3a^2h + 3ah^2 - h^3$ and simplifying, everything cancels except $ah^2 - \frac{h^3}{3}$:
$$V = \boxed{\pi h^2\left(a - \frac{h}{3}\right)}$$

! example | Question V3 — Segment of a sphere
A segment is cut off from a sphere of radius $a$ by a plane at distance $\frac{a}{2}$ from the centre. Show that the volume of the segment is $\frac{5}{32}$ of the volume of the sphere.
**Solution.** The segment is a cap of height $h = a - \frac{a}{2} = \frac{a}{2}$. Directly:
$$\begin{aligned} &V_1 = \pi\int_{a/2}^a (a^2 - x^2)\,dx \\ &= \pi\left[a^2x - \frac{x^3}{3}\right]_{a/2}^a \\ &= \pi\left[\frac{2a^3}{3} - \frac{a^3}{2} + \frac{a^3}{24}\right] \\ &= \frac{5\pi a^3}{24}. \end{aligned}$$
(Check with V2: $\pi\frac{a^2}{4}\left(a - \frac{a}{6}\right) = \frac{5\pi a^3}{24}$.) Volume of sphere $V = \frac{4}{3}\pi a^3$, so $\frac{V_1}{V} = \frac{5/24}{4/3} = \boxed{\frac{5}{32}}$.

! example | Question V4 — Reel from a parabola
The part of the parabola $y^2 = 4ax$ cut off by the latus rectum revolves about the tangent at the vertex. Find the volume of the reel thus generated.
**Solution.**
^ data/net/maths/fig/vol-reel.svg | the arc of $y^2 = 4ax$ from $(a, -2a)$ to $(a, 2a)$ turning about the $y$-axis
The tangent at the vertex is the $y$-axis. The ends of the latus rectum are $(a, \pm 2a)$, so $y$ runs from $-2a$ to $2a$. A strip perpendicular to the $y$-axis has radius $x = \frac{y^2}{4a}$. By symmetry about the $x$-axis:
$$\begin{aligned} &V = 2\int_0^{2a} \pi x^2\,dy \\ &= 2\pi\int_0^{2a} \frac{y^4}{16a^2}\,dy \\ &= \frac{\pi}{8a^2}\left[\frac{y^5}{5}\right]_0^{2a} \\ &= \frac{\pi}{8a^2} \cdot \frac{32a^5}{5} \\ &= \boxed{\frac{4}{5}\pi a^3} \end{aligned}$$
**Note:** the reel is the solid swept by the region between the arc and the axis of rotation (shaded; narrow in the middle like a cotton reel). If instead the region between the arc and the latus rectum were revolved, the volume would be $\pi a^2(4a) - \frac{4}{5}\pi a^3 = \frac{16}{5}\pi a^3$.

! example | Question V5 — Ellipse: mean proportional
Prove that the volume of the solid generated by revolving an ellipse round its minor axis is a mean proportional between the volumes generated by revolving the ellipse and its auxiliary circle about the major axis.
**Solution.** Ellipse $\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$ ($a > b$).
(i) About the minor axis ($y$-axis): $x^2 = a^2\left(1 - \frac{y^2}{b^2}\right)$,
$$\begin{aligned} &V_1 = 2\int_0^b \pi x^2\,dy \\ &= \frac{2\pi a^2}{b^2}\int_0^b (b^2 - y^2)\,dy \\ &= \frac{2\pi a^2}{b^2} \cdot \frac{2b^3}{3} \\ &= \frac{4}{3}\pi a^2b. \end{aligned}$$
(ii) About the major axis: similarly $V_2 = \frac{4}{3}\pi ab^2$. (iii) Auxiliary circle $x^2 + y^2 = a^2$ about the major axis: a sphere, $V_3 = \frac{4}{3}\pi a^3$.
Mean proportional: $\sqrt{V_2V_3} = \frac{4}{3}\pi\sqrt{ab^2 \cdot a^3} = \frac{4}{3}\pi a^2b = V_1$. Hence proved.

! example | Question V6 — Catenary
Find the volume of the solid generated by the revolution of an arc of the catenary $y = c\cosh\frac{x}{c}$ (from $x = 0$ to $x$) about the $x$-axis.
**Solution.**
$$\begin{aligned} &V = \int_0^x \pi c^2\cosh^2\frac{x}{c}\,dx \\ &= \frac{\pi c^2}{2}\int_0^x \left(1 + \cosh\frac{2x}{c}\right)dx \\ &= \boxed{\frac{\pi c^2}{2}\left[x + \frac{c}{2}\sinh\frac{2x}{c}\right]} \end{aligned}$$
(using $\cosh^2 u = \frac{1 + \cosh 2u}{2}$ and $\int \cosh\frac{2x}{c}\,dx = \frac{c}{2}\sinh\frac{2x}{c}$).

## Curves about an asymptote, and loops

! example | Question V7 — Curve about its asymptote
Find the volume of the solid generated by the revolution of the curve $y = \frac{a^3}{a^2 + x^2}$ about its asymptote.
**Solution.**
^ data/net/maths/fig/curve-witch.svg | $y = \frac{a^3}{a^2 + x^2}$, highest point $(0, a)$
As $x \to \pm\infty$, $y \to 0$, so the asymptote is the $x$-axis. The curve is symmetric about the $y$-axis and has its highest point $(0, a)$.
$$\begin{aligned} &V = 2\int_0^\infty \pi y^2\,dx \\ &= 2\pi a^6\int_0^\infty \frac{dx}{(a^2 + x^2)^2}. \end{aligned}$$
Put $x = a\tan\theta$, $dx = a\sec^2\theta\,d\theta$; limits $0 \to \frac{\pi}{2}$:
$$\begin{aligned} &V = 2\pi a^6\int_0^{\pi/2} \frac{a\sec^2\theta}{a^4\sec^4\theta}\,d\theta \\ &= \frac{2\pi a^6}{a^3}\int_0^{\pi/2}\cos^2\theta\,d\theta \\ &= 2\pi a^3 \cdot \frac{\pi}{4} \\ &= \boxed{\frac{\pi^2a^3}{2}} \end{aligned}$$

! example | Question V9 — Loop of the strophoid
Find the volume formed by the revolution of the loop of the curve $y^2(a + x) = x^2(a - x)$ about the $x$-axis.
**Solution.** **Tracing:** symmetric about $x$-axis; $y = 0$ at $x = 0$ (node) and $x = a$; asymptote $x = -a$; the loop lies between $x = 0$ and $x = a$ (see the strophoid in the gallery).
$$\begin{aligned} &V = \int_0^a \pi y^2\,dx \\ &= \pi\int_0^a \frac{ax^2 - x^3}{a + x}\,dx. \end{aligned}$$
Divide: $\frac{-x^3 + ax^2}{x + a} = -x^2 + 2ax - 2a^2 + \frac{2a^3}{x + a}$. Hence
$$\begin{aligned} &V = \pi\Big[-\frac{x^3}{3} + ax^2 - 2a^2x \\ &\qquad + 2a^3\log(a + x)\Big]_0^a \\ &= \pi\Big[-\frac{a^3}{3} + a^3 - 2a^3 \\ &\qquad + 2a^3\log 2\Big] \\ &= \boxed{2\pi a^3\left(\log 2 - \frac{2}{3}\right)} \end{aligned}$$

! example | Question V10 — Loop of $y^2 = x^2(a - x)$
Find the volume of the solid generated by the revolution of the loop of $y^2 = x^2(a - x)$ about the $x$-axis.
**Solution.** Loop between $x = 0$ and $x = a$ (node at origin, see gallery).
$$\begin{aligned} &V = \int_0^a \pi y^2\,dx \\ &= \pi\int_0^a (ax^2 - x^3)\,dx \\ &= \pi\left[\frac{ax^3}{3} - \frac{x^4}{4}\right]_0^a \\ &= \pi\left(\frac{a^4}{3} - \frac{a^4}{4}\right) \\ &= \boxed{\frac{\pi a^4}{12}} \end{aligned}$$

! example | Question V12 / V22 — Astroid
(a) The area of the astroid $x^{2/3} + y^{2/3} = a^{2/3}$ lying in the first quadrant revolves about the $x$-axis. Find the volume. (b) Find the volume of the spindle-shaped solid generated by revolving the whole astroid about the $x$-axis.
**Solution.** Parametric form: $x = a\cos^3\theta$, $y = a\sin^3\theta$; $\frac{dx}{d\theta} = -3a\cos^2\theta\sin\theta$. As $x : 0 \to a$, $\theta : \frac{\pi}{2} \to 0$.
$$\begin{aligned} &V = \int_0^a \pi y^2\,dx \\ &= \pi\int_{\pi/2}^0 a^2\sin^6\theta \\ &\qquad \times (-3a\cos^2\theta\sin\theta)\,d\theta \\ &= 3\pi a^3\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^7\theta\cos^2\theta\,d\theta \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} &= 3\pi a^3 \cdot \frac{6 \cdot 4 \cdot 2 \cdot 1}{9 \cdot 7 \cdot 5 \cdot 3 \cdot 1} \\ &(\text{Wallis; } m = 7 \text{ odd, so no } \tfrac{\pi}{2}) \\ &= 3\pi a^3 \cdot \frac{16}{315} \\ &= \boxed{\frac{16\pi a^3}{105}} \end{aligned}$$
(b) The spindle is twice this (left and right halves): $V = \boxed{\frac{32\pi a^3}{105}}$.

! example | Question V13 — Loop of $a^2y^2 = x^2(2a - x)(x - a)$
Find the volume of the solid obtained by revolving the loop of $a^2y^2 = x^2(2a - x)(x - a)$ about the $x$-axis.
**Solution.** **Tracing:** $y^2 \geq 0$ needs $a \leq x \leq 2a$, so the loop lies between $x = a$ and $x = 2a$ (the origin is an isolated point).
$$\begin{aligned} &V = \frac{\pi}{a^2}\int_a^{2a} x^2(2a - x) \\ &\qquad \times (x - a)\,dx \\ &= \frac{\pi}{a^2}\int_a^{2a}(-x^4 + 3ax^3 \\ &\qquad - 2a^2x^2)\,dx \\ &= \frac{\pi}{a^2}\Big[-\frac{x^5}{5} + \frac{3ax^4}{4} \\ &\qquad - \frac{2a^2x^3}{3}\Big]_a^{2a} \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} &= \frac{\pi}{a^2}a^5\left(-\frac{31}{5} + \frac{45}{4} - \frac{14}{3}\right) \\ &= \frac{\pi a^3}{60}(-372 + 675 - 280) \\ &= \boxed{\frac{23\pi a^3}{60}} \end{aligned}$$

! example | Question V14 — Basin
A basin is formed by the revolution of the curve $x^3 = 64y$ about the axis of $y$. If the depth of the basin is 8 inches, how many cubic inches of water will it hold?
**Solution.**
^ data/net/maths/fig/vol-basin.svg | section of the basin: the curve $x^3 = 64y$ ($x \geq 0$) up to $y = 8$ and its mirror image in the $y$-axis
The strip perpendicular to the $y$-axis has radius $x = (64y)^{1/3}$, so $x^2 = 16y^{2/3}$; $y$ goes from 0 to 8.
$$\begin{aligned} &V = \int_0^8 \pi x^2\,dy \\ &= 16\pi\int_0^8 y^{2/3}\,dy \\ &= 16\pi \cdot \frac{3}{5}\left[y^{5/3}\right]_0^8 \\ &= \frac{48\pi}{5} \cdot 32 \\ &= \boxed{\frac{1536\pi}{5} \text{ cu. in.}} \end{aligned}$$

! example | Question V15 — $(a - x)y^2 = a^2x$ about its asymptote
Show that the volume of the solid generated by the revolution of the curve $(a - x)y^2 = a^2x$ about its asymptote is $\frac{1}{2}\pi^2a^3$.
**Solution.** **Tracing:** coefficient of $y^2$ is $(a - x)$, so $x = a$ is the asymptote; curve exists for $0 \leq x < a$; symmetric about $x$-axis. A strip perpendicular to the asymptote at height $y$ has radius $PM = a - x$ and thickness $\delta y$. From the equation $x = \frac{ay^2}{a^2 + y^2}$, so $a - x = \frac{a^3}{a^2 + y^2}$.
$$\begin{aligned} &V = 2\int_0^\infty \pi(a - x)^2\,dy \\ &= 2\pi a^6\int_0^\infty \frac{dy}{(a^2 + y^2)^2} \\ &\overset{y = a\tan\theta}{=} \frac{2\pi a^6}{a^3}\int_0^{\pi/2}\cos^2\theta\,d\theta \\ &= 2\pi a^3 \cdot \frac{\pi}{4} \\ &= \boxed{\frac{1}{2}\pi^2a^3} \end{aligned}$$

## Cycloids, parametric loops and polar curves

! example | Question V18 — Cycloid with vertex at origin
Find the volume of the solid generated by revolving the cycloid $x = a(\theta + \sin\theta)$, $y = a(1 - \cos\theta)$, $-\pi \leq \theta \leq \pi$ (i) about the $x$-axis (tangent at the vertex), (ii) about its base $y = 2a$.
**Solution.**
^ data/net/maths/fig/vol-cycloid-vertex.svg | the cycloid with its vertex at $O$ and its cusps at $(\pm a\pi, 2a)$
**Table:** $\theta = 0$: $(0, 0)$ vertex; $\theta = \pm\pi$: $(\pm a\pi, 2a)$ cusps. Curve symmetric about the $y$-axis. $\frac{dx}{d\theta} = a(1 + \cos\theta)$.
(i) About the $x$-axis:
$$\begin{aligned} &V = 2\int_0^\pi \pi y^2\frac{dx}{d\theta}\,d\theta \\ &= 2\pi a^3\int_0^\pi (1 - \cos\theta)^2 \\ &\qquad \times (1 + \cos\theta)\,d\theta \\ &= 2\pi a^3\int_0^\pi 4\sin^4\frac{\theta}{2} \\ &\qquad \cdot 2\cos^2\frac{\theta}{2}\,d\theta. \end{aligned}$$
Put $\frac{\theta}{2} = t$:
$$\begin{aligned} &V = 32\pi a^3\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^4 t\cos^2 t\,dt \\ &= 32\pi a^3 \cdot \frac{3 \cdot 1 \cdot 1}{6 \cdot 4 \cdot 2} \cdot \frac{\pi}{2} \\ &= \boxed{\pi^2a^3} \end{aligned}$$
(ii) About $y = 2a$ the radius is $2a - y = a(1 + \cos\theta)$:
$$\begin{aligned} &V = 2\int_0^\pi \pi(2a - y)^2\frac{dx}{d\theta}\,d\theta \\ &= 2\pi a^3\int_0^\pi (1 + \cos\theta)^3\,d\theta \\ &= 16\pi a^3\int_0^\pi \cos^6\frac{\theta}{2}\,d\theta \\ &= 32\pi a^3 \cdot \frac{5 \cdot 3 \cdot 1}{6 \cdot 4 \cdot 2} \cdot \frac{\pi}{2} \\ &= \boxed{5\pi^2a^3} \end{aligned}$$

! example | Question V19 — Same cycloid about the $y$-axis
Show that the volume of the solid generated by the revolution of the cycloid $x = a(\theta + \sin\theta)$, $y = a(1 - \cos\theta)$, $0 \leq \theta \leq \pi$, about the $y$-axis is $\pi a^3\left(\frac{3}{2}\pi^2 - \frac{8}{3}\right)$.
**Solution.** Strip perpendicular to the $y$-axis:
$$\begin{aligned} &V = \int_0^{2a} \pi x^2\,dy \\ &= \int_0^\pi \pi a^2(\theta + \sin\theta)^2\,a\sin\theta\,d\theta \\ &= \pi a^3(I_1 + I_2 + I_3) \end{aligned}$$
where
$$\begin{aligned} &I_1 = \int_0^\pi \theta^2\sin\theta\,d\theta \\ &= \Big[-\theta^2\cos\theta + 2\theta\sin\theta \\ &\qquad + 2\cos\theta\Big]_0^\pi \\ &= \pi^2 - 4; \\ &I_2 = \int_0^\pi \sin^3\theta\,d\theta \\ &= \frac{4}{3}; \\ &I_3 = \int_0^\pi 2\theta\sin^2\theta\,d\theta \\ &= \int_0^\pi \theta(1 - \cos 2\theta)\,d\theta \\ &= \frac{\pi^2}{2} - 0 = \frac{\pi^2}{2}. \end{aligned}$$
$V = \pi a^3\left(\pi^2 - 4 + \frac{4}{3} + \frac{\pi^2}{2}\right) = \boxed{\pi a^3\left(\frac{3}{2}\pi^2 - \frac{8}{3}\right)}$.

! example | Question V21 — Loop of $x = t^2$, $y = t - \frac{t^3}{3}$
Prove that the volume of the solid generated by the revolution about the $x$-axis of the loop of the curve $x = t^2$, $y = t - \frac{1}{3}t^3$ is $\frac{3}{4}\pi$.
**Solution.**
^ data/net/maths/fig/vol-param-loop.svg | the loop of $x = t^2$, $y = t - \frac{t^3}{3}$, closing at $x = 3$
Eliminate $t$:
$$\begin{aligned} &y = t\left(1 - \frac{t^2}{3}\right) \\ &\Rightarrow y^2 = t^2\left(1 - \frac{t^2}{3}\right)^2 \\ &= x\left(1 - \frac{x}{3}\right)^2 \\ &= \frac{x(3 - x)^2}{9}. \end{aligned}$$
$y = 0$ at $x = 0$ and $x = 3$ $\Rightarrow$ loop from 0 to 3.
$$\begin{aligned} &V = \int_0^3 \pi y^2\,dx \\ &= \frac{\pi}{9}\int_0^3 (9x - 6x^2 + x^3)\,dx \\ &= \frac{\pi}{9}\left[\frac{81}{2} - 54 + \frac{81}{4}\right] \\ &= \frac{\pi}{9} \cdot \frac{27}{4} \\ &= \boxed{\frac{3}{4}\pi} \end{aligned}$$

! example | Question V23 — Cissoid about its asymptote
Find the volume of the solid generated by the revolution of the cissoid $x = 2a\sin^2 t$, $y = \frac{2a\sin^3 t}{\cos t}$ about its asymptote.
**Solution.** Cartesian form: $y^2 = \frac{4a^2\sin^6 t}{\cos^2 t} = \frac{x^3}{2a - x}$, i.e. $y^2(2a - x) = x^3$; asymptote $x = 2a$. Strip perpendicular to the asymptote: radius $2a - x = 2a\cos^2 t$, thickness $dy$.
$$\frac{dy}{dt} = 2a\,\frac{3\sin^2 t\cos^2 t + \sin^4 t}{\cos^2 t}.$$
$$\begin{aligned} &V = 2\int_0^\infty \pi(2a - x)^2\,dy \\ &= 2\pi\int_0^{\pi/2} 4a^2\cos^4 t \cdot 2a \\ &\quad \times \frac{\sin^2 t(3\cos^2 t + \sin^2 t)}{\cos^2 t}\,dt \\ &= 16\pi a^3\Big[3\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^2 t\cos^4 t\,dt \\ &\quad + \int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^4 t\cos^2 t\,dt\Big] \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} &= 16\pi a^3\left[3 \cdot \frac{\pi}{32} + \frac{\pi}{32}\right] \\ &= \boxed{2\pi^2a^3} \end{aligned}$$

! example | Question V24 — Circle $r = 2a\cos\theta$
Find the volume of the solid generated by the revolution of $r = 2a\cos\theta$ about the initial line.
**Solution.** $r = 2a\cos\theta$ is the circle on the diameter 0 to $2a$; the upper half is $0 \leq \theta \leq \frac{\pi}{2}$.
$$\begin{aligned} &V = \int_0^{\pi/2} \frac{2}{3}\pi r^3\sin\theta\,d\theta \\ &= \frac{16\pi a^3}{3}\int_0^{\pi/2}\cos^3\theta\sin\theta\,d\theta \\ &= \frac{16\pi a^3}{3} \cdot \frac{1}{4} \\ &= \boxed{\frac{4}{3}\pi a^3} \end{aligned}$$
(a sphere of radius $a$ — a good check).

! example | Question V25 — Cardioid
Find the volume of the solid generated by the revolution of the cardioid $r = a(1 - \cos\theta)$ about the initial line.
**Solution.**
^ data/net/maths/fig/vol-cardioid.svg | the cardioid $r = a(1 - \cos\theta)$, with its cusp at the origin
Upper half: $\theta$ from 0 to $\pi$.
$$\begin{aligned} &V = \int_0^\pi \frac{2}{3}\pi a^3(1 - \cos\theta)^3 \\ &\qquad \times \sin\theta\,d\theta. \end{aligned}$$
Put $u = 1 - \cos\theta$, $du = \sin\theta\,d\theta$, $u : 0 \to 2$:
$$\begin{aligned} &V = \frac{2}{3}\pi a^3\int_0^2 u^3\,du \\ &= \frac{2}{3}\pi a^3 \cdot 4 \\ &= \boxed{\frac{8}{3}\pi a^3} \end{aligned}$$
(Same answer for $r = a(1 + \cos\theta)$.)

## What it means

- The question numbers follow the exercise in the notes; the notes give no V8, V11, V16, V17 or V20, and solve V12 and V22 (the two astroid volumes) together.
- **Checks that catch mistakes**: V24 gives the sphere of radius $a$; the cone, cylinder and sphere examples on the formulas page give the familiar results; a volume can never be negative.
- **About an asymptote** (V7, V15, V23) the integral runs to infinity, but the radius shrinks fast enough for the volume to be finite.

? Volume of a spherical cap of height $h$ from a sphere of radius $a$? :: $\pi h^2\left(a - \frac{h}{3}\right)$.
? Volume of the spindle formed by turning the astroid about the $x$-axis? :: $\frac{32\pi a^3}{105}$.
? Volume formed by the cardioid $r = a(1 + \cos\theta)$ about the initial line? :: $\frac{8}{3}\pi a^3$.
? Volume of the witch $y = \frac{a^3}{a^2 + x^2}$ about its asymptote? :: $\frac{\pi^2a^3}{2}$.

- Volumes of caps, segments, ellipsoids and paraboloids.
- Volumes of loops and of curves about their asymptotes.
- Cycloid, astroid and cardioid volumes, finished with Wallis' formula.

! recap | One-minute revision
- Hemisphere $\frac{2}{3}\pi a^3$; cap $\pi h^2(a - \frac{h}{3})$; reel $\frac{4}{5}\pi a^3$.
- Astroid (whole) $\frac{32}{105}\pi a^3$; cardioid $\frac{8}{3}\pi a^3$; witch and $(a - x)y^2 = a^2x$ about the asymptote $\frac{\pi^2a^3}{2}$.
- Cycloid about the tangent at the vertex $\pi^2a^3$, about the base $5\pi^2a^3$; cissoid about its asymptote $2\pi^2a^3$.
