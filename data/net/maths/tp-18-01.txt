# Last-day rapid revision: 131 one-line facts

> The whole syllabus in 131 one-line facts, chapter by chapter, for the evening before the exam, followed by a plan for using the time in the exam hall. Each fact is worked out on the topic pages; a red box after the facts adds the conditions some of them need.

## Algebra & groups

- (1) Onto maps $5 \to 3$: $150$. One–one $n \to m$: ${}^mP_n$.
- (2) Relations on $n$-set: $2^{n^2}$; reflexive $2^{n^2 - n}$; symmetric $2^{n(n+1)/2}$.
- (3) Equivalence relations on 3-set: $5$; on 4-set: $15$.
- (4) $\sum \frac{n^2}{n!} = 2e$, $\sum \frac{n^3}{n!} = 5e$, $\sum \frac{n^4}{n!} = 15e$.
- (5) Groups of prime order are cyclic, abelian, simple.
- (6) Generators of cyclic group of order $n$: $\varphi(n)$; subgroups: $\tau(n)$.
- (7) $o(a^m) = \frac{n}{\gcd(m, n)}$; in $\mathbb{Z}_n$: $o(k) = \frac{n}{\gcd(k, n)}$.
- (8) Order of permutation = LCM of cycle lengths.
- (9) $r$-cycle $= r - 1$ transpositions; odd-length cycle is even.
- (10) $\lvert S_n \rvert = n!$, $\lvert A_n \rvert = \frac{n!}{2}$; $S_3$ smallest non-abelian.
- (11) Klein 4-group: abelian, not cyclic, every element order $\leq 2$.
- (12) Index-2 subgroup is normal; subgroups of abelian groups are normal.
- (13) $a^2 = e\ \forall a \Rightarrow$ abelian.
- (14) Homomorphisms $\mathbb{Z}_m \to \mathbb{Z}_n$: $\gcd(m, n)$; $\text{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}_n)$: $\varphi(n)$.
- (15) Finite integral domain $\Rightarrow$ field; $\mathbb{Z}_n$ field $\iff n$ prime.
- (16) Field has no proper ideals.
- (17) Fermat: $a^{p-1} \equiv 1 \pmod{p}$ ($p$ prime, $p \nmid a$); Wilson: $(p - 1)! \equiv -1 \pmod{p}$.
- (18) dim of symmetric $n \times n$: $\frac{n(n+1)}{2}$; skew: $\frac{n(n-1)}{2}$.
- (19) Rank + nullity $= \dim V$.
- (20) $\lvert \text{adj}\,A \rvert = \lvert A \rvert^{n-1}$; $\lvert kA \rvert = k^n\lvert A \rvert$.
- (21) Odd-order skew-symmetric determinant $= 0$.
- (22) Eigenvalues: sum = trace, product = det.
- (23) $\rho(A) = \rho(A : B) = n$ unique; $< n$ infinite; unequal none.

## Real analysis

- (24) $\mathbb{Q}$, algebraic numbers, finite subsets of $\mathbb{N}$: countable.
- (25) $\mathbb{R}$, irrationals, transcendentals, $P(\mathbb{N})$, Cantor set: uncountable.
- (26) Cantor set: uncountable, measure $0$, perfect, nowhere dense.
- (27) $\phi$, $\mathbb{R}$: only clopen subsets of $\mathbb{R}$.
- (28) $\bar{\mathbb{Q}} = \mathbb{R}$, $\mathbb{Q}^\circ = \phi$, $\mathbb{Q}' = \mathbb{R}$.
- (29) Compact in $\mathbb{R} \iff$ closed and bounded.
- (30) Bounded sequence has convergent subsequence (B–W).
- (31) Cauchy $\iff$ convergent (in $\mathbb{R}$).
- (32) $\sum \frac{1}{n^p}$ converges iff $p > 1$.
- (33) Ratio $= 1$: use Raabe.
- (34) $n^{1/n} \to 1$; $\frac{(n!)^{1/n}}{n} \to \frac{1}{e}$.
- (35) Differentiable $\Rightarrow$ continuous; not conversely ($\lvert x \rvert$).
- (36) $x^2\sin\frac{1}{x}$: differentiable at $0$, $f'$ not continuous.
- (37) MVT point: $x^2 \to$ AM; $\frac{1}{x} \to$ GM.
- (38) $\lim \frac{\tan x - \sin x}{x^3} = \frac{1}{2}$; $\lim \frac{x - \sin x}{x^3} = \frac{1}{6}$.
- (39) $1^\infty$: $e^{\lim (f - 1)g}$.

- (68) C–R: $u_x = v_y$, $u_y = -v_x$; polar $ru_r = v_\theta$.

- (81) Director circle $x^2 + y^2 = a^2 + b^2$ (ellipse), $a^2 - b^2$ (hyperbola), $2a^2$ (circle $x^2 + y^2 = a^2$).

## Exam-hall strategy

! trick | Exam-hall strategy for speed
- (1) **First pass** ($40\%$ of time): attempt all one-step formula questions (use the facts above). Mark the rest.
- (2) **Option elimination**: put special values ($x = 0, 1$; $a = b$; $n = 1, 2$) into options; check dimensions/symmetry; a probability can never exceed 1 and a variance can never exceed the binomial mean.
- (3) **Don't integrate if a standard result exists**: Wallis, Beta–Gamma, King's rule, Pappus, ready-made areas/volumes.
- (4) **Geometry questions**: classify a conic by $h^2 - ab$ and $\Delta$ before doing any algebra; for 3D distances always use the normal vector directly.
- (5) **Group questions**: reduce to orders of elements and divisibility (Lagrange) — most MCQs fall to $o(a) \mid o(G)$, $\varphi(n)$, $\tau(n)$.
- (6) **Second pass**: lengthy computations; leave guess-work to the very end.

! check | A note on strategy point 2
"A variance can never exceed the binomial mean" is true for binomial variables ($npq \leq np$, as $q \leq 1$); for other distributions the variance can exceed the mean.
