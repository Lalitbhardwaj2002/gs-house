# Vector spaces and subspaces

> A vector space is any set whose elements can be added and multiplied by numbers with the usual rules: arrows in the plane, but also polynomials, matrices and functions. The PGT syllabus asks for examples, the subspace test, sums and direct sums, and quotient spaces.

## Vector spaces and subspaces

! addon | Vector spaces and subspaces
A **vector space** $V$ over a field $F$: $(V, +)$ abelian group, and scalar multiplication with $a(u + v) = au + av$, $(a + b)u = au + bu$, $(ab)u = a(bu)$, $1u = u$.
Standard examples: $F^n$; $M_{m \times n}(F)$ (dim $mn$); polynomials of degree $\leq n$, $P_n$ (dim $n + 1$); $C[a, b]$ (infinite dimensional); $\mathbb{R}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ (infinite dimensional — from the Real notes); $\mathbb{C}$ over $\mathbb{R}$ (dim 2), $\mathbb{C}$ over $\mathbb{C}$ (dim 1).
**Subspace test**: non-empty $W \subseteq V$ is a subspace iff $au + bv \in W$ for all $u, v \in W$, $a, b \in F$. Must contain $0$.
- Intersection of subspaces is a subspace; union $W_1 \cup W_2$ is a subspace iff one contains the other.
- Linear sum $W_1 + W_2 = \{w_1 + w_2\}$ is the smallest subspace containing both. Direct sum $V = W_1 \oplus W_2$ iff $V = W_1 + W_2$ and $W_1 \cap W_2 = \{0\}$.
- $\dim(W_1 + W_2) = \dim W_1 + \dim W_2 - \dim(W_1 \cap W_2)$ ($W_1$, $W_2$ finite-dimensional).
- Quotient space $V/W = \{v + W\}$ with $(u + W) + (v + W) = (u + v) + W$ and $a(v + W) = av + W$; $\dim V/W = \dim V - \dim W$ ($V$ finite-dimensional).

! check | Conditions added
The two dimension formulas hold only in finite dimensions: in the first $W_1$ and $W_2$ must be finite-dimensional, in the second $V$ must be (with infinite dimensions the subtraction means nothing). A quotient space also needs its scalar multiplication $a(v + W) = av + W$; both operations are well defined because $W$ is a subspace.

## What it means

- A **field** is a set where one can add, subtract, multiply and divide (except by 0) with the usual rules: $\mathbb{Q}$, $\mathbb{R}$, $\mathbb{C}$ and $\mathbb{Z}_p$ ($p$ prime) are fields; $\mathbb{Z}$ is not.
- The field matters: $\mathbb{C}$ has dimension 2 over $\mathbb{R}$ (basis $1, i$) but dimension 1 over $\mathbb{C}$ (basis $1$).
- **Quick test for a subspace**: (1) does it contain 0? (2) is it closed under addition? (3) under multiplication by scalars? A set that fails (1) is never a subspace, so check it first.
- In $\mathbb{R}^2$ and $\mathbb{R}^3$ the subspaces are exactly $\{0\}$, the lines and planes **through the origin**, and the whole space.
- In a **direct sum** every vector is $w_1 + w_2$ in exactly one way. For example $\mathbb{R}^2$ is the direct sum of the $x$-axis and the $y$-axis, since $(x, y) = (x, 0) + (0, y)$ and the axes meet only at the origin.
- In a quotient space $V/W$ two vectors give the same element when their difference lies in $W$: $u + W = v + W \iff u - v \in W$. The zero of $V/W$ is the coset $W$ itself. For $V = \mathbb{R}^3$ and $W$ the $z$-axis, $(x, y, z) + W$ depends only on $(x, y)$, so $V/W$ "is" the plane and has dimension $3 - 1 = 2$.
- The dimension formulas for sums and quotients are for **finite-dimensional** spaces.

| Set | Subspace? | Why |
| $\{(x, y) : y = 2x\}$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$ | Yes | a line through the origin |
| $\{(x, y) : y = 2x + 1\}$ | No | does not contain $(0, 0)$ |
| $\{(x, y) : x \geq 0, \ y \geq 0\}$ | No | $(-1)(1, 1)$ leaves the set |
| $x$-axis $\cup$ $y$-axis | No | $(1, 0) + (0, 1)$ leaves the set |
| Symmetric $n \times n$ matrices in $M_n(\mathbb{R})$ | Yes | sums and multiples of symmetric matrices are symmetric |
| Invertible $n \times n$ matrices | No | does not contain the zero matrix |
| Polynomials with $p(1) = 0$ in $P_n$ | Yes | closed under sums and multiples |

! example | Example 1 — a direct sum
Show that $M_n(\mathbb{R}) = S \oplus K$, where $S$ and $K$ are the symmetric and the skew-symmetric matrices.
**Solution.** Every $A = \frac{1}{2}(A + A') + \frac{1}{2}(A - A')$ is a sum of a symmetric and a skew-symmetric matrix, so $M_n = S + K$. A matrix in $S \cap K$ has $A' = A$ and $A' = -A$, so $A = O$. Hence the sum is direct. Dimensions agree: $\frac{n(n+1)}{2} + \frac{n(n-1)}{2} = n^2$.

! example | Example 2 — the dimension of a sum
$W_1$ and $W_2$ are subspaces of $\mathbb{R}^5$ of dimensions 3 and 4. What is the least possible dimension of $W_1 \cap W_2$?
**Solution.** $\dim(W_1 + W_2) \leq 5$, so $\dim(W_1 \cap W_2) = 3 + 4 - \dim(W_1 + W_2) \geq 7 - 5 = \boxed{2}$.

! example | Example 3 — a subspace of polynomials
Show that $W = \{p \in P_2 : p(1) = 0\}$ is a subspace of $P_2$ and find its dimension.
**Solution.** The zero polynomial is in $W$, and $(ap + bq)(1) = ap(1) + bq(1) = 0$. A polynomial with $p(1) = 0$ is $(x - 1)(c + dx)$, so $x - 1$ and $x(x - 1)$ form a basis: $\dim W = \boxed{2} = 3 - 1$.

! example | Example 4 — a quotient space
Let $V = \mathbb{R}^3$ and $W = \{(x, y, z) : x + y + z = 0\}$. Are $(1, 2, 3) + W$ and $(6, 0, 0) + W$ the same element of $V/W$? Find $\dim V/W$.
**Solution.** The difference $(1, 2, 3) - (6, 0, 0) = (-5, 2, 3)$ has coordinate sum 0, so it lies in $W$ and the two cosets are **equal**. In general $(x, y, z) + W = (s, 0, 0) + W$ with $s = x + y + z$, so each coset is fixed by one number $s$ and $(1, 0, 0) + W$ is a basis of $V/W$: $\dim V/W = 3 - 2 = \boxed{1}$.

? Must a subspace contain the zero vector? :: Yes; a set without 0 is never a subspace.
? When is $W_1 \cup W_2$ a subspace? :: Only when one of them contains the other.
? What is the dimension of $\mathbb{C}$ over $\mathbb{R}$? :: 2, with basis $1, i$.
? What is $\dim V/W$? :: $\dim V - \dim W$ (finite dimensions).
? When is $u + W = v + W$ in $V/W$? :: Exactly when $u - v \in W$.

! exam | What the exam asks
- Which of several given sets are subspaces.
- Dimensions of standard spaces and of sums, intersections and quotients.
- Direct sums, especially symmetric and skew-symmetric matrices; equal cosets in a quotient space.

! recap | One-minute revision
- Subspace: contains 0, closed under $au + bv$.
- $\dim(W_1 + W_2) = \dim W_1 + \dim W_2 - \dim(W_1 \cap W_2)$; direct sum when the intersection is $\{0\}$.
- $V/W$: $u + W = v + W \iff u - v \in W$; $\dim V/W = \dim V - \dim W$.
- $\dim P_n = n + 1$, $\dim M_{m \times n} = mn$, $\mathbb{C}$ over $\mathbb{R}$: 2; $\mathbb{R}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$: infinite.
