# Area under and between curves

> The area of a region is the integral of its height. Sketch the curves first, find where they meet, and integrate "upper minus lower" between the meeting points. Both syllabi ask for areas bounded by simple curves.

## Areas

! note | Area under and between curves
^ data/net/maths/fig/two-parabolas.svg | the region between $y^2 = 4ax$ and $x^2 = 4by$, with one thin vertical strip
- Area bounded by $y = f(x)$, the $x$-axis and $x = a$, $x = b$: $\int_a^b \lvert y \rvert\,dx$.
- By $x = g(y)$, the $y$-axis and $y = c$, $y = d$: $\int_c^d \lvert x \rvert\,dy$.
- Between two curves: $\int_a^b (y_{\text{upper}} - y_{\text{lower}})\,dx$, where $a, b$ are the $x$-coordinates of the intersection points.
- Polar: $\frac{1}{2}\int_\alpha^\beta r^2\,d\theta$. Parametric: $\int y\frac{dx}{dt}\,dt$.

! check | Conditions added
"Upper minus lower" needs the two curves not to cross between $a$ and $b$; if they do, split the interval at the crossings, i.e. integrate $\lvert y_1 - y_2 \rvert$. The polar formula gives the area swept by the radius from $\theta = \alpha$ to $\theta = \beta$. In the parametric formula take the $t$-limits so that $x$ increases (or take the absolute value), with $y \geq 0$.

! example | Question AR1 — standard
Find the area enclosed by $y^2 = 4ax$ and $x^2 = 4ay$.
**Solution.** Intersections: $x^4 = 16a^2y^2 = 64a^3x \Rightarrow x = 0, 4a$. Area
$$\begin{aligned} &= \int_0^{4a}\left(2\sqrt{ax} - \frac{x^2}{4a}\right)dx \\ &= \left[\frac{4}{3}\sqrt{a}\,x^{3/2} - \frac{x^3}{12a}\right]_0^{4a} \\ &= \frac{32a^2}{3} - \frac{16a^2}{3} = \boxed{\frac{16a^2}{3}}. \end{aligned}$$

! example | Question AR2 — standard
Find the area of the ellipse $\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$.
**Solution.** By symmetry, $4\int_0^a \frac{b}{a}\sqrt{a^2 - x^2}\,dx = \frac{4b}{a} \cdot \frac{\pi a^2}{4} = \boxed{\pi ab}$.

! example | Question AR3 — standard
Find the area bounded by $y = x^2$ and $y = x$.
**Solution.** Meet at $x = 0, 1$; $y = x$ is above. $\int_0^1 (x - x^2)\,dx = \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{3} = \boxed{\frac{1}{6}}$.

! trick | Shortcut / time-saver — Ready-made areas
Ellipse $\pi ab$. $y^2 = 4ax$ and its latus rectum: $\frac{8a^2}{3}$. $y^2 = 4ax$ and $y = mx$: $\frac{8a^2}{3m^3}$. Parabolic segment $= \frac{2}{3} \times$ enclosing rectangle. One arch of $\sin x$: 2. Cycloid arch $3\pi a^2$; astroid $\frac{3}{8}\pi a^2$; cardioid $\frac{3}{2}\pi a^2$.

! check | Conditions added
Here, as in AR1, $a > 0$; and $m > 0$ for the line $y = mx$ (for $m < 0$ the area is $\frac{8a^2}{3\lvert m \rvert^3}$). The curves are the cycloid $x = a(t - \sin t)$, $y = a(1 - \cos t)$ (area under one arch), the astroid $x^{2/3} + y^{2/3} = a^{2/3}$ and the cardioid $r = a(1 + \cos\theta)$.

## What it means

- **Steps**: sketch; find the points of intersection; decide which curve is above (or to the right); integrate the difference; use symmetry to halve or quarter the work.
- **Strips the other way**: if the region is easier to describe as "right curve minus left curve", integrate with respect to $y$.
- **Signs**: where a curve lies below the $x$-axis, $\int y\,dx$ is negative; split the interval at the crossings and add the absolute values.
- The **general $y^2 = 4ax$, $x^2 = 4by$** of the figure ($a, b > 0$) meet at $x = 0$ and $x = 4a^{1/3}b^{2/3}$ and enclose $\frac{16ab}{3}$; Question AR1 is the case $b = a$.
- **Polar areas** use thin sectors of area $\frac{1}{2}r^2\,d\theta$ instead of strips.

! example | Example 1 — a parabola and its latus rectum
Find the area bounded by $y^2 = 4x$ and the line $x = 1$.
**Solution.** $2\int_0^1 2\sqrt{x}\,dx = 4 \cdot \frac{2}{3} = \boxed{\frac{8}{3}}$, as the shortcut $\frac{8a^2}{3}$ with $a = 1$ gives.

! example | Example 2 — strips along $y$
Find the area between $x = y^2$ and $x = 2 - y^2$.
**Solution.** They meet where $y^2 = 2 - y^2$, i.e. $y = \pm 1$. Right minus left: $\int_{-1}^1 (2 - 2y^2)\,dy = 4 - \frac{4}{3} = \boxed{\frac{8}{3}}$.

! example | Example 3 — a polar area
Find the area of the cardioid $r = a(1 + \cos\theta)$.
**Solution.** $(1 + \cos\theta)^2 = 1 + 2\cos\theta + \cos^2\theta$, whose three terms integrate over $[0, 2\pi]$ to $2\pi$, $0$ and $\pi$:
$$\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{2}\int_0^{2\pi} a^2(1 + \cos\theta)^2\,d\theta \\ &= \frac{a^2}{2}\left(2\pi + 0 + \pi\right) = \boxed{\frac{3}{2}\pi a^2}. \end{aligned}$$

? Area between two curves from $x = a$ to $x = b$? :: $\int_a^b (y_{\text{upper}} - y_{\text{lower}})\,dx$.
? Area of the ellipse $\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$? :: $\pi ab$.
? Area bounded by $y = x^2$ and $y = x$? :: $\frac{1}{6}$.
? Area of a polar region? :: $\frac{1}{2}\int_\alpha^\beta r^2\,d\theta$.

! exam | What the exam asks
- Areas between a parabola and a line, two parabolas, or a curve and an axis.
- Areas of the ellipse, the circle, and standard curves (cycloid, astroid, cardioid).
- Choosing vertical or horizontal strips; using symmetry.

! recap | One-minute revision
- Area $= \int (\text{upper} - \text{lower})\,dx$ between the intersection points.
- Ellipse $\pi ab$; $y^2 = 4ax$ with its latus rectum $\frac{8a^2}{3}$; $y = x^2$ and $y = x$: $\frac{1}{6}$.
- Polar $\frac{1}{2}\int r^2\,d\theta$; parametric $\int y\frac{dx}{dt}\,dt$.
