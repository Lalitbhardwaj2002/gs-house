# Increasing and decreasing functions

> The sign of the derivative tells where a function rises and where it falls. Factorise $f'(x)$, mark its zeros, and read the sign on each interval.

## Increasing and decreasing

! note | Increasing and decreasing functions
^ data/net/maths/fig/rise-fall.svg | $f' > 0$ where the curve rises, $f' < 0$ where it falls
- Increasing: $x_1 < x_2 \Rightarrow f(x_1) \leq f(x_2)$ (strictly: $<$). Decreasing: $\geq$ (strictly: $>$).
- If $f$ is continuous on $[a, b]$ and differentiable on $(a, b)$: $f' > 0 \Rightarrow$ increasing; $f' < 0 \Rightarrow$ decreasing; $f' = 0 \Rightarrow$ constant.
- Geometrically: the tangent slopes upwards where $f$ rises and downwards where it falls.

! example | Question ID1 — from the notes
Show that $f(x) = x^3 - 3x^2 + 4x$ is strictly increasing on $\mathbb{R}$.
**Solution.** $f'(x) = 3x^2 - 6x + 4 = 3(x - 1)^2 + 1 > 0$ for all $x$. Hence strictly increasing on $\mathbb{R}$.

! example | Question ID2 — from the notes
Find the intervals in which $f(x) = 4x^3 - 6x^2 - 72x + 30$ is strictly increasing or decreasing.
**Solution.**
^ data/net/maths/fig/sign-scheme.svg | sign scheme of $f'(x)$
$f'(x) = 12x^2 - 12x - 72 = 12(x - 3)(x + 2)$; zeros $x = -2, 3$. Sign of $f'$: $+$ on $(-\infty, -2)$, $-$ on $(-2, 3)$, $+$ on $(3, \infty)$. Strictly increasing on $\boxed{(-\infty, -2) \cup (3, \infty)}$, strictly decreasing on $\boxed{(-2, 3)}$.

! example | Question ID3 — from the notes
Find where (a) $f(x) = \sin 3x$ on $\left[0, \frac{\pi}{2}\right]$, (b) $f(x) = \sin x + \cos x$ on $[0, 2\pi]$ is increasing or decreasing.
**Solution.** (a) $f' = 3\cos 3x = 0$ at $3x = \frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{3\pi}{2}$, i.e. $x = \frac{\pi}{6}, \frac{\pi}{2}$. Increasing on $\left[0, \frac{\pi}{6}\right]$, decreasing on $\left[\frac{\pi}{6}, \frac{\pi}{2}\right]$. (b) $f' = \cos x - \sin x = 0$ at $x = \frac{\pi}{4}, \frac{5\pi}{4}$. Strictly increasing on $\left[0, \frac{\pi}{4}\right) \cup \left(\frac{5\pi}{4}, 2\pi\right]$, strictly decreasing on $\left(\frac{\pi}{4}, \frac{5\pi}{4}\right)$.

! trick | Shortcut / time-saver
Sign scheme: factorise $f'$, mark its zeros on a number line; the rightmost interval has the sign of the leading coefficient; the sign alternates across simple zeros and does not change across squared factors.

## What it means

- **Strict and non-strict**: $f' > 0$ on an interval is enough for strictly increasing, but not necessary: $x^3$ is strictly increasing on $\mathbb{R}$ although $f'(0) = 0$. A derivative that vanishes only at isolated points does not spoil strict monotonicity.
- **On an interval only**: $f(x) = -\frac{1}{x}$ has $f' > 0$ wherever it is defined, yet $f(-1) = 1 > f(1) = -1$. The rule applies to each interval of the domain separately.
- **Monotone functions are one–one**: a strictly increasing or decreasing function never takes a value twice, so it has an inverse on that interval.
- **Proving inequalities**: to show $g(x) > h(x)$ for $x > 0$, let $f = g - h$; if $f(0) = 0$ and $f' > 0$ for $x > 0$, then $f(x) > 0$.

! example | Example 1 — an inequality
Show that $\log(1 + x) < x$ for $x > 0$.
**Solution.** Let $f(x) = x - \log(1 + x)$. Then $f(0) = 0$ and $f'(x) = 1 - \frac{1}{1 + x} = \frac{x}{1 + x} > 0$ for $x > 0$. So $f$ is strictly increasing and $f(x) > 0$, i.e. $\log(1 + x) < x$.

! example | Example 2 — a parameter
For which $a$ is $f(x) = x^3 + ax^2 + 3x$ increasing on $\mathbb{R}$?
**Solution.** $f'(x) = 3x^2 + 2ax + 3 \geq 0$ for all $x$ needs discriminant $4a^2 - 36 \leq 0$, so $\boxed{-3 \leq a \leq 3}$.

! example | Example 3 — a squared factor
Where is $f$ increasing if $f'(x) = (x - 1)^2(x - 4)$?
**Solution.** $(x - 1)^2 \geq 0$ does not change sign; $f' > 0$ exactly for $x > 4$ (and $f' \leq 0$ before 4). So $f$ is increasing on $[4, \infty)$ and decreasing on $(-\infty, 4]$.

? What does $f' > 0$ on an interval tell you? :: $f$ is strictly increasing on that interval.
? Is $x^3$ strictly increasing on $\mathbb{R}$, although $f'(0) = 0$? :: Yes; a derivative vanishing at isolated points does not matter.
? Where is $\sin x + \cos x$ decreasing on $[0, 2\pi]$? :: On $\left(\frac{\pi}{4}, \frac{5\pi}{4}\right)$.
? How does the sign of $f'$ behave at a squared factor? :: It does not change there.

! exam | What the exam asks
- Intervals of increase and decrease of polynomials and trigonometric functions.
- Values of a parameter for which a function is monotonic on $\mathbb{R}$.
- Inequalities proved by the sign of a derivative.

! recap | One-minute revision
- $f' > 0$: increasing; $f' < 0$: decreasing; $f' = 0$ on an interval: constant.
- Sign scheme: zeros of $f'$, leading coefficient on the right, alternate at simple zeros.
- Apply the rule on each interval of the domain separately.
