# Sequences and series: AP, GP, HP

> In an arithmetic progression each term is the previous one plus a fixed number; in a geometric progression, the previous one times a fixed number; a harmonic progression is one whose reciprocals are in AP. Questions ask for a term, a sum, or the means between two numbers.

## The formulas

! addon | AP, GP, HP and the means
- **AP**: $T_n = a + (n-1)d$, $S_n = \frac{n}{2}[2a + (n-1)d] = \frac{n}{2}(a + l)$. Three terms in AP: $a - d, \ a, \ a + d$.
- **GP**: $T_n = ar^{n-1}$, $S_n = \frac{a(r^n - 1)}{r - 1}$ ($r \neq 1$), $S_\infty = \frac{a}{1 - r}$ ($\lvert r \rvert < 1$). Three terms: $\frac{a}{r}, \ a, \ ar$.
- **HP**: reciprocals form an AP.
- Means of positive $a, b$: $A = \frac{a+b}{2}$, $G = \sqrt{ab}$, $H = \frac{2ab}{a+b}$; $A \geq G \geq H$ and $G^2 = AH$.
- $\sum n = \frac{n(n+1)}{2}$, $\sum n^2 = \frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6}$, $\sum n^3 = \left[\frac{n(n+1)}{2}\right]^2$.

! check | Conditions added: $r \neq 1$, and $A \geq G \geq H$
The GP sum $S_n = \frac{a(r^n - 1)}{r - 1}$ needs $r \neq 1$ (it would divide by 0); for $r = 1$ every term is $a$ and $S_n = na$. The inequality $A \geq G \geq H$ holds for **positive** $a$ and $b$, with equality only when $a = b$. For negative numbers it can fail: with $a = b = -1$, $G = \sqrt{ab} = 1$ is greater than $A = -1$.

## What it means

- In an AP, $d = T_2 - T_1$ is the **common difference**; in a GP, $r = \frac{T_2}{T_1}$ is the **common ratio**. Three numbers $a, b, c$ are in AP if $2b = a + c$, in GP if $b^2 = ac$, and in HP if $b = \frac{2ac}{a + c}$.
- The sum of an AP is (number of terms) $\times$ (average of first and last term). For a GP, use $S_n = \frac{a(1 - r^n)}{1 - r}$ when $r < 1$, to keep the numbers positive; if $r = 1$, $S_n = na$.
- An infinite GP has a sum only when $\lvert r \rvert < 1$, because then $r^n \to 0$.
- **Inserting means**: $n$ arithmetic means between $a$ and $b$ have $d = \frac{b - a}{n + 1}$ and sum $n \cdot \frac{a + b}{2}$; $n$ geometric means between positive $a$ and $b$ have $r = \left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{1/(n+1)}$ and product $(\sqrt{ab})^n$.
- There is no formula for the sum of an HP. Work with the reciprocals, which are in AP: the $n$th term of the HP is $\frac{1}{\frac{1}{a} + (n-1)d}$.
- **Arithmetico-geometric series** $a + (a + d)r + (a + 2d)r^2 + \cdots$ with $\lvert r \rvert < 1$: $S_\infty = \frac{a}{1 - r} + \frac{dr}{(1 - r)^2}$.

! example | Question SQ1 — standard
The 3rd term of an AP is 7 and the 7th term is 15. Find the sum of the first 20 terms.
**Solution.** $a + 2d = 7$, $a + 6d = 15 \Rightarrow d = 2$, $a = 3$. $S_{20} = \frac{20}{2}[6 + 19 \cdot 2] = 10 \cdot 44 = \boxed{440}$.

! example | Question SQ2 — standard
Find the sum to infinity of $8 + 4 + 2 + 1 + \cdots$ and of $\sum_{k=1}^{10} k^2$.
**Solution.** $S_\infty = \frac{8}{1 - \frac{1}{2}} = \boxed{16}$. $\sum_{k=1}^{10} k^2 = \frac{10 \cdot 11 \cdot 21}{6} = \boxed{385}$.

! trick | Shortcut / time-saver
If $S_n$ is given, $T_n = S_n - S_{n-1}$ (for $n \geq 2$; $T_1 = S_1$). If $S_n = An^2 + Bn$, the series is an AP with $d = 2A$.

! check | The first term
$T_n = S_n - S_{n-1}$ is for $n \geq 2$; the first term is $T_1 = S_1$. The formula also gives $T_1$ only when it makes $S_0 = 0$. For $S_n = n^2 + 1$: $T_1 = S_1 = 2$, then $T_n = 2n - 1$ for $n \geq 2$, so the series $2, 3, 5, 7, \dots$ is not an AP.

## More worked examples

! example | Example 1 — a GP sum
Find the sum of the first 6 terms of $3 + 6 + 12 + \cdots$.
**Solution.** $a = 3$, $r = 2$: $S_6 = \frac{3(2^6 - 1)}{2 - 1} = 3 \cdot 63 = \boxed{189}$.

! example | Example 2 — the three means
Find the AM, GM and HM of 4 and 16 and check that $G^2 = AH$.
**Solution.** $A = 10$, $G = \sqrt{64} = 8$, $H = \frac{2 \cdot 64}{20} = 6.4$. $AH = 64 = G^2$, and $10 \geq 8 \geq 6.4$.

! example | Example 3 — a term of an HP
The 3rd term of an HP is $\frac{1}{7}$ and the 7th term is $\frac{1}{15}$. Find the 20th term.
**Solution.** The reciprocals 7, 15 are the 3rd and 7th terms of an AP: as in SQ1, $a = 3$, $d = 2$. Its 20th term is $3 + 19 \cdot 2 = 41$, so the 20th term of the HP is $\boxed{\frac{1}{41}}$.

! example | Example 4 — a series with $\sum n^2$
Find the sum of $1 \cdot 2 + 2 \cdot 3 + 3 \cdot 4 + \cdots$ up to $n$ terms.
**Solution.** $T_k = k(k+1) = k^2 + k$. So $S_n = \frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6} + \frac{n(n+1)}{2} = \frac{n(n+1)}{6}(2n + 1 + 3) = \boxed{\frac{n(n+1)(n+2)}{3}}$.

! example | Example 5 — an arithmetico-geometric series
Find $1 + \frac{2}{3} + \frac{3}{9} + \frac{4}{27} + \cdots$ to infinity.
**Solution.** $a = 1$, $d = 1$, $r = \frac{1}{3}$: $S_\infty = \frac{1}{1 - \frac{1}{3}} + \frac{\frac{1}{3}}{\left(1 - \frac{1}{3}\right)^2} = \frac{3}{2} + \frac{3}{4} = \boxed{\frac{9}{4}}$.

! example | Example 6 — from $S_n$ to $T_n$
The sum of $n$ terms of a series is $3n^2 + 5n$. Find its $n$th term.
**Solution.** $T_n = S_n - S_{n-1} = (3n^2 + 5n) - (3(n-1)^2 + 5(n-1)) = \boxed{6n + 2}$: an AP with $d = 6 = 2 \times 3$.

? Sum of the first $n$ cubes? :: $\left[\frac{n(n+1)}{2}\right]^2$, the square of the sum of the first $n$ natural numbers.
? When does an infinite GP have a sum, and what is it? :: When $\lvert r \rvert < 1$; the sum is $\frac{a}{1 - r}$.
? How are the AM, GM and HM of two positive numbers related? :: $A \geq G \geq H$ and $G^2 = AH$.
? If $S_n = An^2 + Bn$, what kind of series is it? :: An AP with common difference $2A$.

! exam | What the exam asks
- A term or a sum of an AP or GP from two given terms; the sum to infinity of a GP.
- Means between two numbers and the relation $G^2 = AH$.
- Sums of series built from $\sum n$, $\sum n^2$, $\sum n^3$.

! recap | One-minute revision
- AP: $T_n = a + (n-1)d$, $S_n = \frac{n}{2}(a + l)$. GP: $T_n = ar^{n-1}$, $S_\infty = \frac{a}{1 - r}$.
- HP: reciprocals in AP. Positive $a, b$: $A \geq G \geq H$, $G^2 = AH$.
- $\sum n$, $\sum n^2$, $\sum n^3$: $\frac{n(n+1)}{2}$, $\frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6}$, $\left[\frac{n(n+1)}{2}\right]^2$.
