# Centre of gravity

> The centre of gravity is the point through which the whole weight acts: a weighted mean of positions, a sum for separate masses and an integral for a continuous body. Symmetry gives part of the answer at once; a table of standard positions gives the rest. A body resting on a curved surface is stable if a small tilt raises its centre of gravity.

## Finding the centre of gravity

! note | Centre of gravity
- **CG**: the point through which the whole weight of the body acts. For a uniform body CG = centroid. It lies on every axis of symmetry; usually inside the body, but it can lie outside for hollow or non-convex bodies (ring, hollow sphere, L-shaped plate).
- **Discrete masses**: $\bar{x} = \frac{\sum mx}{\sum m}$, $\bar{y} = \frac{\sum my}{\sum m}$. **Continuous**: $\bar{x} = \frac{\int x\,dm}{\int dm}$.
- **Arc**: $\bar{x} = \frac{\int x\,ds}{\int ds}$, $\bar{y} = \frac{\int y\,ds}{\int ds}$; $ds = \sqrt{1 + y'^2}\,dx = \sqrt{r^2 + r_1^2}\,d\theta = \sqrt{\dot{x}^2 + \dot{y}^2}\,dt$.
- **Plane area** under $y = f(x)$: $\bar{x} = \frac{\int xy\,dx}{\int y\,dx}$, $\bar{y} = \frac{\int \frac{y}{2} \cdot y\,dx}{\int y\,dx}$ (CG of the strip is at its middle, height $\frac{y}{2}$). Region symmetric about the $x$-axis $\Rightarrow \bar{y} = 0$.
- **Region** $x = f(y)$: $\bar{x} = \frac{\int \frac{x}{2}x\,dy}{\int x\,dy}$, $\bar{y} = \frac{\int yx\,dy}{\int x\,dy}$. **Polar sector**: $\bar{x} = \frac{\int \frac{2}{3}r^3\cos\theta\,d\theta}{\int r^2\,d\theta}$, $\bar{y} = \frac{\int \frac{2}{3}r^3\sin\theta\,d\theta}{\int r^2\,d\theta}$.
- **Solid of revolution** about the $x$-axis: $\bar{x} = \frac{\int x\,\pi y^2\,dx}{\int \pi y^2\,dx}$, $\bar{y} = 0$ (CG lies on the axis).

! check | Conditions added
These formulas are for a **uniform** body (constant density); otherwise put the density into $dm$. In the arc formula $r_1 = \frac{dr}{d\theta}$. The area formulas take $y \geq 0$ (strips from the $x$-axis up to the curve). In the polar-sector formula the region is bounded by the curve $r = f(\theta)$ and two radii from the pole, and $\frac{2}{3}r$ is the distance of the CG of a thin triangle-like sector from the pole.

! example | Question CG1 — from the notes
Find the CG of the arc of the astroid $x^{2/3} + y^{2/3} = a^{2/3}$ lying in the first quadrant.
**Solution.**
^ data/net/maths/fig/mech-cg-astroid.svg | the quarter of the astroid in the first quadrant and its CG $G\left(\frac{2a}{5}, \frac{2a}{5}\right)$
$x = a\cos^3 t$, $y = a\sin^3 t$; $\frac{ds}{dt} = \sqrt{9a^2\cos^4 t\sin^2 t + 9a^2\sin^4 t\cos^2 t} = 3a\sin t\cos t$.
Length $s = \int_0^{\pi/2} 3a\sin t\cos t\,dt = \frac{3a}{2}$.
$$\begin{aligned} &\bar{x} = \\ &\frac{\int_0^{\pi/2} a\cos^3 t \cdot 3a\sin t\cos t\,dt}{3a/2} \\ &= \frac{3a^2 \cdot \frac{1}{5}}{3a/2} = \boxed{\frac{2a}{5}} \end{aligned}$$
The arc is symmetric about $y = x$, so $\bar{y} = \bar{x} = \boxed{\frac{2a}{5}}$.

! example | Question CG2 — from the notes
Find the CG of the arc of the cardioid $r = a(1 + \cos\theta)$ lying above the initial line.
**Solution.** $\frac{ds}{d\theta} = \sqrt{r^2 + r_1^2} = 2a\cos\frac{\theta}{2}$; $s = \int_0^{\pi} 2a\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\,d\theta = 4a$.
$x = r\cos\theta = 2a\cos^2\frac{\theta}{2}\left(2\cos^2\frac{\theta}{2} - 1\right)$, $y = r\sin\theta = 4a\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\cos^3\frac{\theta}{2}$. With $\frac{\theta}{2} = t$ ($d\theta = 2\,dt$):
$$\begin{aligned} &\int x\,ds \\ &= 8a^2\int_0^{\pi/2}(2\cos^5 t - \cos^3 t)\,dt \\ &= 8a^2\left(\frac{16}{15} - \frac{2}{3}\right) = \frac{16a^2}{5}, \end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned} &\int y\,ds \\ &= 16a^2\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin t\cos^4 t\,dt \\ &= \frac{16a^2}{5}. \end{aligned}$$
$$\bar{x} = \frac{16a^2/5}{4a} = \boxed{\frac{4a}{5}},$$
$$\bar{y} = \boxed{\frac{4a}{5}}.$$

! example | Question CG3 — from the notes
Find the CG of a solid right circular cone of height $h$.
**Solution.**
^ data/net/maths/fig/mech-cg-cone.svg | the cone with vertex $O$ on the $x$-axis: $G$ is $\frac{3h}{4}$ from the vertex, $\frac{h}{4}$ from the base
Place the vertex at $O$, axis along the $x$-axis; the generating line is $y = x\tan\alpha$ ($\alpha$ semi-vertical angle).
$$\begin{aligned} \bar{x} &= \frac{\int_0^h x \cdot \pi x^2\tan^2\alpha\,dx}{\int_0^h \pi x^2\tan^2\alpha\,dx} \\ &= \frac{h^4/4}{h^3/3} = \boxed{\frac{3h}{4}} \end{aligned}$$
from the vertex, i.e. $\boxed{\frac{h}{4}}$ from the base.

! example | Question CG4
Find the CG of the area bounded by the parabola $y^2 = 4ax$ and its latus rectum.
**Solution.** The area is symmetric about the $x$-axis $\Rightarrow \bar{y} = 0$. Take strips of height $2y = 4\sqrt{ax}$ from $x = 0$ to $a$:
$$\begin{aligned} \bar{x} &= \frac{\int_0^a x \cdot 4\sqrt{a}\,x^{1/2}\,dx}{\int_0^a 4\sqrt{a}\,x^{1/2}\,dx} \\ &= \frac{\frac{2}{5}a^{5/2}}{\frac{2}{3}a^{3/2}} = \boxed{\frac{3a}{5}}. \end{aligned}$$
For only the upper half: $\bar{x} = \frac{3a}{5}$, $\bar{y} = \frac{\int_0^a \frac{y^2}{2}\,dx}{\int_0^a y\,dx} = \frac{a^3}{\frac{4}{3}a^2} = \boxed{\frac{3a}{4}}$.

! example | Question CG5 — standard
Find the CG of (i) a semicircular lamina, (ii) a solid hemisphere, of radius $a$.
**Solution.** (i) Lamina $x^2 + y^2 \leq a^2$, $y \geq 0$:
$$\begin{aligned} \bar{y} &= \frac{\int_{-a}^{a} \frac{y}{2}\,y\,dx}{\pi a^2/2} \\ &= \frac{\frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{4a^3}{3}}{\pi a^2/2} = \boxed{\frac{4a}{3\pi}} \end{aligned}$$
from the centre.
(ii) Hemisphere by revolving the quadrant about the $x$-axis:
$$\begin{aligned} \bar{x} &= \frac{\int_0^a x\,\pi(a^2 - x^2)\,dx}{\frac{2}{3}\pi a^3} \\ &= \frac{\pi a^4/4}{2\pi a^3/3} = \boxed{\frac{3a}{8}} \end{aligned}$$
from the centre.

! note | Standard positions of CG — learn by heart
| Body | Position of the CG |
| Uniform rod | midpoint |
| Rectangle, parallelogram, circle | centre (intersection of diagonals) |
| Triangle (lamina) | intersection of medians: $\frac{h}{3}$ from base |
| Semicircular arc / lamina | $\frac{2a}{\pi}$ / $\frac{4a}{3\pi}$ from centre |
| Circular arc of angle $2\alpha$ / sector | $\frac{a\sin\alpha}{\alpha}$ / $\frac{2a\sin\alpha}{3\alpha}$ from centre |
| Solid cone / hollow cone | $\frac{h}{4}$ / $\frac{h}{3}$ from base |
| Solid hemisphere / hollow hemisphere | $\frac{3a}{8}$ / $\frac{a}{2}$ from centre |

## Stability of equilibrium

! addon | Stability of equilibrium (from the notes)
A body (radius of curvature $\rho_2$) rests on a fixed rough body ($\rho_1$), CG at height $h$ above the point of contact, common normal inclined at $\alpha$ to the vertical. Stable if $h < \frac{\rho_1\rho_2\cos\alpha}{\rho_1 + \rho_2}$, unstable if $>$. When $\alpha = 0$: stable iff $\frac{1}{h} > \frac{1}{\rho_1} + \frac{1}{\rho_2}$. Body on a plane ($\rho_1 = \infty$): stable iff $h < \rho_2$. If the height of the CG is a maximum ($z'' < 0$), the equilibrium is unstable.

! check | Conditions added
These results are for two surfaces convex towards each other at the point of contact, rough enough for the upper body to roll without slipping. If the body rests inside a hollow (concave) fixed surface, $\rho_1$ is taken negative. At the borderline value of $h$ a closer test is needed. "Stable" means that after a small displacement the body returns.

! example | Question St1 — standard
A solid hemisphere rests on a horizontal plane with its curved surface in contact. Is the equilibrium stable?
**Solution.** $\rho_2 = a$ (radius), plane: $\rho_1 = \infty$. CG is $\frac{3a}{8}$ from the centre, so its height above the point of contact is $h = a - \frac{3a}{8} = \frac{5a}{8} < a$. Hence **stable**.

## What it means

- **CG as a weighted mean**: $\bar{x}$ is the average of the $x$-coordinates, each counted with its mass; for a continuous body the sum becomes an integral.
- **Use symmetry first**: every axis of symmetry passes through the CG, which often gives one coordinate at once (CG1, CG4).
- **Strip method**: cut the area into thin strips whose own CG is known (the middle of a vertical strip, at height $\frac{y}{2}$), then take the weighted mean of those points.
- **Stability**: if a small tilt raises the CG, the weight pulls the body back (stable); if it lowers the CG, the body topples (unstable).

! example | Example 1 — separate masses
Masses of $1$, $2$ and $3$ kg are placed at $(0, 0)$, $(2, 0)$ and $(0, 3)$. Find their CG.
**Solution.** $\bar{x} = \frac{1 \cdot 0 + 2 \cdot 2 + 3 \cdot 0}{6} = \frac{2}{3}$, $\bar{y} = \frac{3 \cdot 3}{6} = \frac{3}{2}$: $\boxed{G\left(\frac{2}{3}, \frac{3}{2}\right)}$.

! example | Example 2 — a disc with a hole
From a uniform disc of radius $2a$ a disc of radius $a$, touching it internally, is cut out. Find the CG of the remainder.
**Solution.** Treat the hole as a negative mass. Areas $4\pi a^2$ (centre $O$) and $\pi a^2$ (centre at distance $a$). $\bar{x} = \frac{4\pi a^2 \cdot 0 - \pi a^2 \cdot a}{4\pi a^2 - \pi a^2} = -\frac{a}{3}$: the CG is $\boxed{\frac{a}{3}}$ from $O$, on the side away from the hole.

? CG of a solid hemisphere of radius $a$? :: $\frac{3a}{8}$ from the centre.
? CG of a solid cone of height $h$? :: $\frac{h}{4}$ from the base ($\frac{3h}{4}$ from the vertex).
? CG of a semicircular lamina and of a semicircular arc? :: $\frac{4a}{3\pi}$ and $\frac{2a}{\pi}$ from the centre.
? A body with radius of curvature $\rho_2$ on a plane is stable if? :: $h < \rho_2$.

! exam | What the exam asks
- The table of standard positions (cones, hemispheres, arcs, sectors).
- CG of an arc or an area by integration (astroid, cardioid, parabola).
- Stability of a body resting on a plane or on another body.

! recap | One-minute revision
- $\bar{x} = \frac{\sum mx}{\sum m}$; arc: weight $ds$; area: strip $y\,dx$ with CG at $\frac{y}{2}$; solid: $\pi y^2\,dx$.
- Hemisphere $\frac{3a}{8}$, cone $\frac{h}{4}$ from the base, semicircle $\frac{4a}{3\pi}$, arc $\frac{2a}{\pi}$.
- Stable on a plane iff $h < \rho_2$; in general $\frac{1}{h} > \frac{1}{\rho_1} + \frac{1}{\rho_2}$.
