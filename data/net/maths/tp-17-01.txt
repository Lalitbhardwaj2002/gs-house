# Work and time

> Turn every worker or pipe into a rate, work done per day or per hour. Rates add when people work together (and subtract for a pipe that empties); the time is the whole work divided by the combined rate.

## Work, time and pipes

! addon | Work and time
- If A finishes a work in $a$ days, A's one day's work is $\frac{1}{a}$.
- A and B together: $\frac{1}{a} + \frac{1}{b}$ per day, so $\frac{ab}{a + b}$ days. A, B, C together: $\frac{abc}{ab + bc + ca}$ days.
- If A and B together take $x$ days and A alone $a$ days, B alone takes $\frac{ax}{a - x}$ days.
- $\frac{M_1D_1H_1}{W_1} = \frac{M_2D_2H_2}{W_2}$ (men × days × hours per unit of work).
- Pipes: a filling pipe counts $+\frac{1}{a}$ per hour, an emptying pipe $-\frac{1}{b}$.
- Wages are shared in the ratio of work done.

! check | Conditions added
Each worker keeps a steady rate, and in the $M_1D_1H_1$ rule all workers are equally efficient. In $\frac{ax}{a - x}$, $a > x$ (working together is quicker). A tank with a filling and an emptying pipe fills only if the filling rate is the larger.

! example | Question W1 — standard
A can do a work in $12$ days and B in $18$ days. In how many days will they finish it together?
**Solution.** LCM method: total work $= 36$ units; A does $3$, B does $2$ units per day. Together $5$ units/day: $\frac{36}{5} = \boxed{7\frac{1}{5} \text{ days}}$.

! example | Question W2 — standard
A pipe fills a tank in $6$ hours and another empties it in $9$ hours. If both are opened, when is the tank full?
**Solution.** Net per hour $\frac{1}{6} - \frac{1}{9} = \frac{1}{18}$: $\boxed{18 \text{ hours}}$.

## What it means

- **Rates add, times do not**: two workers taking $12$ and $18$ days do not take $15$ or $30$ days together; their daily fractions $\frac{1}{12}$ and $\frac{1}{18}$ add.
- **The LCM method** avoids fractions: take the total work as the LCM of the days, so every daily rate is a whole number of units.
- **Wages follow work**: if they work for the same number of days, the shares are in the ratio of the daily rates.

! example | Example 1 — sharing wages
A can do a job in $10$ days and B in $15$ days. Working together they earn ₹1500. Find each share.
**Solution.** Rates $\frac{1}{10} : \frac{1}{15} = 3 : 2$. A gets $\frac{3}{5} \times 1500 = $ ₹900, B gets ₹600.

! example | Example 2 — men, days and hours
$12$ men working $8$ hours a day finish a work in $10$ days. In how many days will $16$ men working $6$ hours a day finish it?
**Solution.** $12 \cdot 10 \cdot 8 = 16 \cdot D \cdot 6 \Rightarrow D = \boxed{10}$ days.

? A alone $a$ days, B alone $b$ days: together? :: $\frac{ab}{a + b}$ days.
? A and B together $x$ days, A alone $a$ days: B alone? :: $\frac{ax}{a - x}$ days.
? Rule for men, days and hours? :: $\frac{M_1D_1H_1}{W_1} = \frac{M_2D_2H_2}{W_2}$.

! exam | What the exam asks
- Time taken together; one worker leaving part way.
- Pipes and cisterns with filling and emptying pipes.
- Sharing wages; men-days-hours problems.

! recap | One-minute revision
- One day's work $= \frac{1}{\text{days}}$; rates add.
- Together: $\frac{ab}{a + b}$; three: $\frac{abc}{ab + bc + ca}$.
- $M_1D_1H_1W_2 = M_2D_2H_2W_1$.
