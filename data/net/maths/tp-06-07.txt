# Curvature, asymptotes and curve tracing

> Tracing a curve means sketching it from its equation: symmetry, intercepts, the region where it exists, asymptotes and turning points. Curvature measures how sharply it bends. The TGT syllabus asks for the tracing of simple curves.

## Radius of curvature

! addon | Radius of curvature
^ data/net/maths/fig/curvature-circle.svg | the circle of curvature at $P$, of radius $\rho$
The circle of curvature at $P$ fits the curve most closely; its radius is
$$\rho = \frac{ds}{d\psi} = \frac{(1 + y_1^2)^{3/2}}{y_2}, \qquad \rho = \frac{(\dot{x}^2 + \dot{y}^2)^{3/2}}{\dot{x}\ddot{y} - \dot{y}\ddot{x}}, \qquad \rho = \frac{(r^2 + r_1^2)^{3/2}}{r^2 + 2r_1^2 - rr_2}.$$
Standard values: circle $a$; parabola $y^2 = 4ax$ at the vertex $2a$; ellipse at the end of the major axis $\frac{b^2}{a}$; catenary $\frac{y^2}{c}$; cardioid $\frac{2}{3}\sqrt{2ar}$.

## Tracing a curve

! addon | Curve tracing checklist
- (1) **Symmetry**: only even powers of $y \to$ about the $x$-axis; only even powers of $x \to$ about the $y$-axis; unchanged under $(x, y) \to (-x, -y) \to$ opposite quadrants; under $x \leftrightarrow y \to$ about $y = x$.
- (2) **Origin**: no constant term $\Rightarrow$ passes through the origin; tangents there = lowest-degree terms equated to 0.
- (3) **Intercepts** and tangents at them. **Region**: where $y^2 < 0$ there is no curve.
- (4) **Asymptotes**: parallel to the $x$-axis: coefficient of the highest power of $x = 0$; parallel to the $y$-axis: coefficient of the highest power of $y = 0$; oblique $y = mx + c$: $\phi_n(m) = 0$, $c = -\frac{\phi_{n-1}(m)}{\phi_n'(m)}$.
- (5) **Monotonicity**, max/min, points of inflection ($y'' = 0$ with a sign change).

## What it means

- **Curvature** $\kappa = \frac{1}{\rho}$ is the rate at which the tangent turns per unit length of arc. A straight line has $\kappa = 0$ ($\rho = \infty$); a circle of radius $a$ has $\rho = a$ everywhere. In the first formula take $\lvert y_2 \rvert$ when only the size of $\rho$ is wanted.
- **Asymptote**: a straight line that the curve approaches as it goes to infinity. In the notation of the checklist, $\phi_n(m)$ is what the terms of highest degree $n$ become when $x = 1$, $y = m$, and $\phi_{n-1}(m)$ the same for the terms of degree $n - 1$.
- **Points of inflection** are where the curve changes from bending upwards ($y'' > 0$) to bending downwards ($y'' < 0$) or back.
- **Polar curves**: use symmetry in $\theta$ (if $r(\theta) = r(-\theta)$ the curve is symmetric about the initial line) and tabulate $r$ for the angles $0, \frac{\pi}{6}, \frac{\pi}{4}, \frac{\pi}{3}, \frac{\pi}{2}, \dots$

! example | Example 1 — radius of curvature
Find the radius of curvature of $y = x^2$ at the origin.
**Solution.** $y_1 = 2x = 0$ and $y_2 = 2$ at the origin, so $\rho = \frac{(1 + 0)^{3/2}}{2} = \boxed{\frac{1}{2}}$. (The curve is the parabola $x^2 = 4ay$ with $a = \frac{1}{4}$, whose radius at the vertex is $2a = \frac{1}{2}$ ✓.)

! example | Example 2 — asymptotes of a rational curve
Find the asymptotes of $y = \frac{2x + 1}{x - 3}$.
**Solution.** As $x \to 3$, $y \to \pm\infty$: vertical asymptote $\boxed{x = 3}$. As $x \to \pm\infty$, $y \to 2$: horizontal asymptote $\boxed{y = 2}$.

! example | Example 3 — tracing $y^2 = x^3$ (semi-cubical parabola)
Describe the curve $y^2 = x^3$.
**Solution.** Only $y^2$ appears: symmetric about the $x$-axis. It passes through the origin, where the lowest-degree term $y^2 = 0$ gives the $x$-axis as a double tangent (a cusp). $y^2 = x^3 < 0$ for $x < 0$, so the curve lies in $x \geq 0$, and $\lvert y \rvert$ grows without bound as $x$ grows; there are no asymptotes.

? Radius of curvature in Cartesian form? :: $\rho = \frac{(1 + y_1^2)^{3/2}}{y_2}$.
? Radius of curvature of $y^2 = 4ax$ at its vertex? :: $2a$.
? When is a curve symmetric about the $x$-axis? :: When its equation contains only even powers of $y$.
? Area of the cardioid $r = a(1 + \cos\theta)$? :: $\frac{3}{2}\pi a^2$.
