# Simple and compound interest

> Simple interest grows by the same amount every year; compound interest earns interest on interest, so the amount is multiplied by $\left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)$ every period. The same multiplier describes growth and depreciation.

## Simple and compound interest

! addon | Simple and compound interest
- $\text{SI} = \frac{PRT}{100}$, amount $A = P + \text{SI}$.
- CI: $A = P\left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^T$; half-yearly $P\left(1 + \frac{R}{200}\right)^{2T}$; quarterly $P\left(1 + \frac{R}{400}\right)^{4T}$; different yearly rates: $P\left(1 + \frac{R_1}{100}\right)\left(1 + \frac{R_2}{100}\right)\cdots$.
- $\text{CI} - \text{SI}$ for 2 years $= P\left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2$; for 3 years $= P\left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2\left(3 + \frac{R}{100}\right)$.
- Growth/depreciation: $P\left(1 \pm \frac{R}{100}\right)^n$. Rule of 72: money doubles in about $\frac{72}{R}$ years.
- Effective annual rate for nominal $r$ compounded $k$ times: $\left(1 + \frac{r}{k}\right)^k - 1$.

! check | Conditions added
$R$ is the rate per cent per year and $T$ the time in years; in the effective-rate formula $r$ is a decimal (e.g. $0.12$). The rule of 72 is an approximation, good for rates of about $4\%$ to $15\%$.

! example | Question CI1 — standard
Find the difference between CI and SI on Rs 10,000 for 2 years at $10\%$ per annum.
**Solution.** CI: $10000(1.1)^2 - 10000 = 2100$; SI $= 2000$; difference $= \boxed{\text{Rs } 100} = 10000(0.1)^2$ ✓.

! example | Question CI2 — standard
The population of a town is $50{,}000$ and grows at $4\%$ per year. Find it after 2 years.
**Solution.** $50000(1.04)^2 = 50000 \times 1.0816 = \boxed{54{,}080}$.

## What it means

- **Why CI $-$ SI $= P\left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2$ for two years**: in the second year compound interest also earns interest on the first year's interest, $P \cdot \frac{R}{100}$, at the rate $\frac{R}{100}$.
- **More frequent compounding** gives a slightly larger amount: the effective rate exceeds the nominal rate.
- **Depreciation** is compound interest backwards: the value is multiplied by $\left(1 - \frac{R}{100}\right)$ each year.

! example | Example 1 — three years
Find CI $-$ SI on ₹10,000 for 3 years at $10\%$.
**Solution.** $10000 \times (0.1)^2 \times (3 + 0.1) = \boxed{₹310}$.

! example | Example 2 — the effective rate
Find the effective annual rate of $12\%$ per annum compounded quarterly.
**Solution.** $(1.03)^4 - 1 = 1.1255 - 1 \approx \boxed{12.55\%}$.

! example | Example 3 — depreciation
A machine worth ₹50,000 depreciates by $10\%$ a year. Find its value after 2 years.
**Solution.** $50000(0.9)^2 = \boxed{₹40{,}500}$.

? Simple interest? :: $\frac{PRT}{100}$.
? Compound amount, half-yearly? :: $P\left(1 + \frac{R}{200}\right)^{2T}$.
? CI $-$ SI for 2 years? :: $P\left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2$.
? Rule of 72? :: Money doubles in about $\frac{72}{R}$ years.

! exam | What the exam asks
- CI and SI; their difference for 2 and 3 years.
- Half-yearly and quarterly compounding; different rates in different years.
- Population growth and depreciation.

! recap | One-minute revision
- SI $= \frac{PRT}{100}$; CI amount $P\left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^T$.
- CI $-$ SI: $P\left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2$ (2 yrs), $\times\left(3 + \frac{R}{100}\right)$ (3 yrs).
- Effective rate $\left(1 + \frac{r}{k}\right)^k - 1$.
