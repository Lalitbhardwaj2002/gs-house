# Surfaces: all questions of the exercise, fully solved

> The surface swept when an arc turns about an axis is $\int 2\pi(\text{distance from the axis})\,ds$. Every question below finds $\frac{ds}{dx}$, $\frac{ds}{d\theta}$ or $\frac{ds}{dt}$ first; then the integral usually collapses to a power of a sine or cosine, finished by Wallis' formula.

## Spheres, parabolas, catenaries and ellipses

! example | Question S1 — Sphere
Find the surface of a sphere of radius $a$.
**Solution.** Revolve the semicircle $y^2 = a^2 - x^2$ about the $x$-axis. $2y\frac{dy}{dx} = -2x \Rightarrow \frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{x}{y}$, so $1 + \left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^2 = \frac{x^2 + y^2}{y^2} = \frac{a^2}{y^2}$ and $\frac{ds}{dx} = \frac{a}{y}$.
$$S = 2\int_0^a 2\pi y \cdot \frac{a}{y}\,dx = 4\pi a\int_0^a dx = \boxed{4\pi a^2}$$

! example | Question S2 — Spherical zone
Show that the surface of the spherical zone contained between two parallel planes is $2\pi ah$, where $a$ is the radius of the sphere and $h$ the distance between the planes.
**Solution.** Let the planes be $x = b$ and $x = b + h$. As in S1, $y\frac{ds}{dx} = a$, so
$$S = \int_b^{b+h} 2\pi y\frac{ds}{dx}\,dx = 2\pi a\int_b^{b+h} dx = \boxed{2\pi ah}$$
It does not depend on $b$: every zone of the same height has the same area.

! example | Question S3 — Parabola
Find the area of the surface formed by the revolution of the parabola $y^2 = 4ax$ about the $x$-axis by the arc from the vertex to one end of the latus rectum.
**Solution.** $2y\frac{dy}{dx} = 4a \Rightarrow \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{2a}{y}$;
$$1 + \frac{4a^2}{y^2} = \frac{4ax + 4a^2}{y^2} = \frac{4a(x + a)}{y^2}, \quad \text{so } \frac{ds}{dx} = \frac{\sqrt{4a(x + a)}}{y}.$$
$$\begin{aligned} S &= \int_0^a 2\pi y\,\frac{\sqrt{4a(x + a)}}{y}\,dx \\ &= 4\pi\sqrt{a}\int_0^a (x + a)^{1/2}\,dx \\ &= 4\pi\sqrt{a} \cdot \frac{2}{3}\left[(2a)^{3/2} - a^{3/2}\right] \\ &= \boxed{\frac{8}{3}\pi a^2(2\sqrt{2} - 1)} \end{aligned}$$

! example | Question S4 — Catenary
Find the surface generated by the revolution of an arc of the catenary $y = c\cosh\frac{x}{c}$ about the $x$-axis between the planes $x = a$ and $x = b$.
**Solution.** $\frac{dy}{dx} = \sinh\frac{x}{c}$, $1 + \sinh^2\frac{x}{c} = \cosh^2\frac{x}{c}$, so $\frac{ds}{dx} = \cosh\frac{x}{c}$.
$$\begin{aligned} S &= \int_a^b 2\pi c\cosh^2\frac{x}{c}\,dx \\ &= \pi c\int_a^b \left(1 + \cosh\frac{2x}{c}\right)dx \\ &= \boxed{\pi c\left[(b - a) + \frac{c}{2}\left(\sinh\frac{2b}{c} - \sinh\frac{2a}{c}\right)\right]} \end{aligned}$$

! example | Question S6 — Ellipse about its major axis
Find the surface of the solid generated by the revolution of the ellipse $x^2 + 4y^2 = 16$ about its major axis.
**Solution.** $\frac{x^2}{16} + \frac{y^2}{4} = 1$: $a = 4$, $b = 2$, major axis = $x$-axis. $2x + 8y\frac{dy}{dx} = 0 \Rightarrow \frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{x}{4y}$;
$$\frac{ds}{dx} = \frac{\sqrt{16y^2 + x^2}}{4y} = \frac{\sqrt{4(16 - x^2) + x^2}}{4y} = \frac{\sqrt{64 - 3x^2}}{4y}.$$
$$\begin{aligned} S &= 2\int_0^4 2\pi y\,\frac{\sqrt{64 - 3x^2}}{4y}\,dx \\ &= \pi\int_0^4 \sqrt{8^2 - (\sqrt{3}x)^2}\,dx \\ &= \frac{\pi}{\sqrt{3}} \cdot \frac{1}{2}\left[\sqrt{3}x\sqrt{64 - 3x^2} + 64\sin^{-1}\frac{\sqrt{3}x}{8}\right]_0^4 \\ &= \frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{3}}\left[4\sqrt{3} \cdot 4 + 64 \cdot \frac{\pi}{3}\right] \\ &= \boxed{8\pi\left[1 + \frac{4\pi}{3\sqrt{3}}\right]} \end{aligned}$$

! example | Question S7 — Semi-cubical parabola about the $y$-axis
Find the surface of the solid formed by the revolution about the $y$-axis of the part of the curve $ay^2 = x^3$ from $x = 0$ to $x = 4a$ above the $x$-axis.
**Solution.** $2ay\frac{dy}{dx} = 3x^2 \Rightarrow$
$$1 + \left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^2 = 1 + \frac{9x^4}{4a^2y^2} = 1 + \frac{9x^4}{4ax^3} = \frac{4a + 9x}{4a}.$$
$$S = \int_0^{4a} 2\pi x\frac{ds}{dx}\,dx = \frac{\pi}{\sqrt{a}}\int_0^{4a} x\sqrt{4a + 9x}\,dx.$$
Put $4a + 9x = t^2$, $dx = \frac{2t}{9}\,dt$, $x = \frac{t^2 - 4a}{9}$; $t : 2\sqrt{a} \to 2\sqrt{10a}$:
$$\begin{aligned} S &= \frac{2\pi}{81\sqrt{a}}\left[\frac{t^5}{5} - \frac{4at^3}{3}\right]_{2\sqrt{a}}^{2\sqrt{10a}} \\ &= \frac{2\pi}{81\sqrt{a}} \cdot \frac{a^2\sqrt{a}(8000\sqrt{10} + 64)}{15} \\ &= \boxed{\frac{128\pi a^2}{1215}\left(125\sqrt{10} + 1\right)} \end{aligned}$$

## Cycloids, loops and polar curves

! example | Question S8 — Cycloid about its base
Find the surface area of the solid generated by revolving the cycloid $x = a(\theta - \sin\theta)$, $y = a(1 - \cos\theta)$ about its base.
**Solution.**
$$\frac{ds}{d\theta} = \sqrt{a^2(1 - \cos\theta)^2 + a^2\sin^2\theta} = a\sqrt{2(1 - \cos\theta)} = 2a\sin\frac{\theta}{2}.$$
One arch: $\theta : 0 \to 2\pi$ (or twice $0 \to \pi$).
$$\begin{aligned} S &= 2\int_0^\pi 2\pi \cdot 2a\sin^2\tfrac{\theta}{2} \cdot 2a\sin\tfrac{\theta}{2}\,d\theta = 16\pi a^2\int_0^\pi \sin^3\tfrac{\theta}{2}\,d\theta \\ &\overset{\theta/2 = t}{=} 32\pi a^2\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^3 t\,dt = 32\pi a^2 \cdot \frac{2}{3} = \boxed{\frac{64}{3}\pi a^2} \end{aligned}$$

! example | Question S9 — Cycloid with vertex at origin
Find the area of the surface generated by revolving an arch of the cycloid $x = a(\theta + \sin\theta)$, $y = a(1 - \cos\theta)$ about the $x$-axis.
**Solution.** $\frac{dx}{d\theta} = a(1 + \cos\theta)$, $\frac{dy}{d\theta} = a\sin\theta$, $\frac{ds}{d\theta} = a\sqrt{2(1 + \cos\theta)} = 2a\cos\frac{\theta}{2}$.
$$\begin{aligned} S &= 2\int_0^\pi 2\pi a(1 - \cos\theta)\,2a\cos\tfrac{\theta}{2}\,d\theta = 16\pi a^2\int_0^\pi \sin^2\tfrac{\theta}{2}\cos\tfrac{\theta}{2}\,d\theta \\ &= 32\pi a^2\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^2 t\cos t\,dt = \boxed{\frac{32}{3}\pi a^2} \end{aligned}$$

! example | Question S10 — Surface : area $= 64 : 9$
The portion between two consecutive cusps of the cycloid $x = a(\theta + \sin\theta)$, $y = a(1 + \cos\theta)$ is revolved about the $x$-axis. Prove that the area of the surface so formed is to the area of the cycloid as $64 : 9$.
**Solution.**
^ data/net/maths/fig/surf-cycloid-arch.svg | the arch between the cusps $(\pm a\pi, 0)$, top $(0, 2a)$
Cusps at $\theta = \pm\pi$ ($y = 0$), top at $\theta = 0$ $(0, 2a)$. $\frac{ds}{d\theta} = 2a\cos\frac{\theta}{2}$.
$$\begin{aligned} S &= 2\int_0^\pi 2\pi a(1 + \cos\theta)2a\cos\tfrac{\theta}{2}\,d\theta = 16\pi a^2\int_0^\pi \cos^3\tfrac{\theta}{2}\,d\theta \\ &= 32\pi a^2 \cdot \frac{2}{3} = \frac{64}{3}\pi a^2. \end{aligned}$$
Area:
$$A = 2\int_0^\pi y\frac{dx}{d\theta}\,d\theta = 2a^2\int_0^\pi (1 + \cos\theta)^2\,d\theta = 2a^2\left[\pi + 0 + \frac{\pi}{2}\right] = 3\pi a^2.$$
$\frac{S}{A} = \frac{64\pi a^2/3}{3\pi a^2} = \frac{64}{9}$, i.e. $S : A = 64 : 9$. Hence proved.

! example | Question S11 — Loop of $x = t^2$, $y = t - \frac{t^3}{3}$
Prove that the surface area of the solid generated by the revolution, about the $x$-axis, of the loop of the curve $x = t^2$, $y = t - \frac{1}{3}t^3$ is $3\pi$.
**Solution.** From V21, $y^2 = \frac{x(3 - x)^2}{9}$. Differentiating:
$$2y\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{(3 - x)^2 - 2x(3 - x)}{9} = \frac{(3 - x)(3 - 3x)}{9} \Rightarrow \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{(3 - x)(1 - x)}{6y}.$$
$$\begin{aligned} 1 + \left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^2 &= \frac{36y^2 + (3 - x)^2(1 - x)^2}{36y^2} \\ &= \frac{(3 - x)^2[4x + (1 - x)^2]}{36y^2} = \frac{(3 - x)^2(1 + x)^2}{36y^2}. \end{aligned}$$
So $y\frac{ds}{dx} = \frac{(3 - x)(1 + x)}{6}$ and
$$S = \int_0^3 2\pi y\frac{ds}{dx}\,dx = \frac{\pi}{3}\int_0^3 (3 + 2x - x^2)\,dx = \frac{\pi}{3}[9 + 9 - 9] = \boxed{3\pi}$$

! example | Question S13 — Circle $r = 2a\cos\theta$
Find the area of the surface of revolution formed by revolving the curve $r = 2a\cos\theta$ about the initial line.
**Solution.** $\frac{dr}{d\theta} = -2a\sin\theta$, $r^2 + r_1^2 = 4a^2$, so $\frac{ds}{d\theta} = 2a$. Upper half: $0 \leq \theta \leq \frac{\pi}{2}$.
$$\begin{aligned} S &= \int_0^{\pi/2} 2\pi(r\sin\theta) \cdot 2a\,d\theta = 8\pi a^2\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin\theta\cos\theta\,d\theta \\ &= 4\pi a^2\left[-\frac{1}{2}\cos 2\theta\right]_0^{\pi/2} = \boxed{4\pi a^2} \end{aligned}$$

! example | Question S14 — Cardioid about the initial line
Find the surface of the solid formed by the revolution of the cardioid $r = a(1 + \cos\theta)$ about the initial line.
**Solution.** $r^2 + r_1^2 = a^2(1 + \cos\theta)^2 + a^2\sin^2\theta = 2a^2(1 + \cos\theta) = 4a^2\cos^2\frac{\theta}{2}$, so $\frac{ds}{d\theta} = 2a\cos\frac{\theta}{2}$.
$$\begin{aligned} S &= \int_0^\pi 2\pi a(1 + \cos\theta)\sin\theta \cdot 2a\cos\tfrac{\theta}{2}\,d\theta \\ &= 4\pi a^2\int_0^\pi 2\cos^2\tfrac{\theta}{2} \cdot 2\sin\tfrac{\theta}{2}\cos\tfrac{\theta}{2} \cdot \cos\tfrac{\theta}{2}\,d\theta \\ &= 16\pi a^2\int_0^\pi \cos^4\tfrac{\theta}{2}\sin\tfrac{\theta}{2}\,d\theta \\ &= 32\pi a^2\int_0^{\pi/2}\cos^4 t\sin t\,dt = 32\pi a^2 \cdot \frac{1}{5} = \boxed{\frac{32}{5}\pi a^2} \end{aligned}$$

! example | Question S15 — Cardioid about $\theta = \frac{\pi}{2}$
The arc of the cardioid $r = a(1 + \cos\theta)$ included between $-\frac{\pi}{2} \leq \theta \leq \frac{\pi}{2}$ is rotated about the line $\theta = \frac{\pi}{2}$. Find the area of the surface generated.
**Solution.** Distance from the line $\theta = \frac{\pi}{2}$ is $x = r\cos\theta$; $\frac{ds}{d\theta} = 2a\cos\frac{\theta}{2}$. By symmetry take twice $0 \to \frac{\pi}{2}$:
$$\begin{aligned} S &= 2\int_0^{\pi/2} 2\pi a(1 + \cos\theta)\cos\theta \cdot 2a\cos\tfrac{\theta}{2}\,d\theta \\ &\overset{\theta = 2t}{=} 32\pi a^2\int_0^{\pi/4}\left(2\cos^5 t - \cos^3 t\right)dt. \end{aligned}$$
With $s = \sin t$: $\int\cos^3 t = s - \frac{s^3}{3}$, $\int\cos^5 t = s - \frac{2s^3}{3} + \frac{s^5}{5}$. At $t = \frac{\pi}{4}$, $s = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$:
$$\begin{aligned} &2\left(s - \frac{2s^3}{3} + \frac{s^5}{5}\right) - \left(s - \frac{s^3}{3}\right) = s - s^3 + \frac{2s^5}{5} \\ &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(1 - \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{10}\right) = \frac{3}{5\sqrt{2}}. \end{aligned}$$
$$S = 32\pi a^2 \cdot \frac{3}{5\sqrt{2}} = \boxed{\frac{48\sqrt{2}}{5}\pi a^2}$$

! check | About S15 in the notebook
The notebook integrates from $0$ to $\pi$ and writes $\frac{32}{5}\pi a^2$. With the limits in the question ($-\frac{\pi}{2}$ to $\frac{\pi}{2}$) the correct value is $\frac{48\sqrt{2}}{5}\pi a^2$, as worked above.

! trick | Shortcut / time-saver — Ready answers for MCQs
- Sphere: $V = \frac{4}{3}\pi a^3$, $S = 4\pi a^2$
- Zone: $2\pi ah$; cap: $\pi h^2\left(a - \frac{h}{3}\right)$
- Cycloid (base): $V = 5\pi^2a^3$, $S = \frac{64}{3}\pi a^2$
- Cycloid (tangent at vertex): $V = \pi^2a^3$, $S = \frac{32}{3}\pi a^2$
- Cardioid (initial line): $V = \frac{8}{3}\pi a^3$, $S = \frac{32}{5}\pi a^2$
- Astroid (full, $x$-axis): $V = \frac{32}{105}\pi a^3$, $S = \frac{12}{5}\pi a^2$
- Witch $y = \frac{a^3}{a^2 + x^2}$ and $(a - x)y^2 = a^2x$, each about its asymptote: $\frac{\pi^2a^3}{2}$
- Cissoid about asymptote: $2\pi^2a^3$
- Torus (Pappus): $V = 2\pi^2a^2b$, $S = 4\pi^2ab$
- Ellipse: $\frac{4}{3}\pi ab^2$ (major), $\frac{4}{3}\pi a^2b$ (minor)

## What it means

- **Which $ds$?** Cartesian: $ds = \sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^2}\,dx$. Parametric: $ds = \sqrt{\left(\frac{dx}{dt}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{dy}{dt}\right)^2}\,dt$. Polar: $ds = \sqrt{r^2 + r_1^2}\,d\theta$ with $r_1 = \frac{dr}{d\theta}$; the distance from the initial line is $y = r\sin\theta$.
- **About the $x$-axis** the radius is $y$; **about the $y$-axis** it is $x$; about any other line it is the distance from that line (as in S15, where it is $x = r\cos\theta$).
- **Archimedes' zone result** (S2): a sphere and the cylinder wrapped round it cut off equal areas between any two parallel planes; with $h = 2a$ this gives the whole sphere, $4\pi a^2$.
- **Pappus' theorems**: a plane area $A$ turned about an axis it does not cross gives volume $A \times 2\pi\bar{y}$; an arc of length $L$ gives surface $L \times 2\pi\bar{y}$, where $\bar{y}$ is the distance of the centroid from the axis.
- **A quick check**: for a sphere, $\frac{d}{da}\left(\frac{4}{3}\pi a^3\right) = 4\pi a^2$; the surface is the rate at which the volume grows with the radius.

! example | Example 1 — the torus by Pappus
A circle of radius $a$ turns about a line in its plane at distance $b$ ($b > a$) from its centre. Find the volume and the surface of the ring.
**Solution.** The centroid of the disc and of the circle is the centre, at distance $b$; it travels $2\pi b$. Volume $= \pi a^2 \cdot 2\pi b = \boxed{2\pi^2a^2b}$. Surface $= 2\pi a \cdot 2\pi b = \boxed{4\pi^2ab}$.

! example | Example 2 — the curved surface of a cone
Show by integration that the curved surface of a cone of base radius $r$ and height $h$ is $\pi rl$, where $l = \sqrt{r^2 + h^2}$.
**Solution.** Turn the line $y = \frac{r}{h}x$, $0 \leq x \leq h$, about the $x$-axis. $\frac{ds}{dx} = \sqrt{1 + \frac{r^2}{h^2}} = \frac{l}{h}$.
$$S = \int_0^h 2\pi \cdot \frac{r}{h}x \cdot \frac{l}{h}\,dx = \frac{2\pi rl}{h^2} \cdot \frac{h^2}{2} = \boxed{\pi rl}$$

! example | Example 3 — the astroid
Find the surface formed by turning the astroid $x = a\cos^3\theta$, $y = a\sin^3\theta$ about the $x$-axis.
**Solution.** $\frac{dx}{d\theta} = -3a\cos^2\theta\sin\theta$, $\frac{dy}{d\theta} = 3a\sin^2\theta\cos\theta$, so $\frac{ds}{d\theta} = 3a\sin\theta\cos\theta$ for $0 \leq \theta \leq \frac{\pi}{2}$. The two halves ($x > 0$ and $x < 0$) give equal surfaces:
$$S = 2\int_0^{\pi/2} 2\pi a\sin^3\theta \cdot 3a\sin\theta\cos\theta\,d\theta = 12\pi a^2\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^4\theta\cos\theta\,d\theta = \boxed{\frac{12}{5}\pi a^2}$$

? Surface of a sphere of radius $a$, and of a zone of height $h$ on it? :: $4\pi a^2$; $2\pi ah$, whatever the position of the zone.
? Surface formed by turning one arch of the cycloid $x = a(\theta - \sin\theta)$, $y = a(1 - \cos\theta)$ about its base? :: $\frac{64}{3}\pi a^2$.
? Surface of the cardioid $r = a(1 + \cos\theta)$ about the initial line? :: $\frac{32}{5}\pi a^2$.
? Volume and surface of a torus (tube radius $a$, centre circle radius $b$)? :: $2\pi^2a^2b$ and $4\pi^2ab$ (Pappus).
