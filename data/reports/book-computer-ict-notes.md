# Computer and ICT notes of the book companion: fact-check of 13 November 2026

Files checked: `data/book/gc-comp-01.txt` to `gc-comp-06.txt` and `gc-ict-01.txt` to `gc-ict-04.txt`. Every change was made in both the English and the Hindi part. The structure check prints "same" for all ten files.

## gc-comp-01 (basic concepts, number systems, data units)
- ENIAC is now "the first general-purpose electronic **digital** computer" (EN and HI). This is the standard wording.
- Hindi: "10 का मान 1010" changed to "10 का बाइनरी रूप 1010". The decimal value of 10 is not 1010; its binary form is.
- No other errors. The Hindi unit mnemonic is a different trick from the English one. It was kept because it teaches the same order.

## gc-comp-02 (memory, storage, file formats)
- No errors found. The first hard disk (IBM, 1956), the EPROM and EEPROM erasing methods and the CD/DVD/Blu-ray capacities are correct.

## gc-comp-03 (devices, software, languages)
- No errors found. Engelbart (mouse), Ritchie (C), Gosling (Java) and Linux (1991) are correct.

## gc-comp-04 (networking)
- Ring topology: "Data moves in one direction" changed to "Data **usually** moves in one direction", because dual and bidirectional rings exist.
- ARPANET: the vague "first network of its kind" changed to "the first wide-area packet-switching network".
- Recap: "mesh is the safest" changed to "mesh is the most reliable", so that it matches the table.

## gc-comp-05 (Internet, web, protocols)
- Domain table: ".edu, .ac" changed to ".edu, .ac.in". On its own, .ac is the country code of Ascension Island.
- UDP: "does not check" changed to "does not guarantee delivery". UDP does have a checksum.
- Hindi recap: "उस पर के पृष्ठ" changed to the more natural "उस पर रखे पृष्ठ".
- The timeline is correct: ARPANET 1969, first e-mail 1971, TCP/IP on 1 January 1983, WWW proposal 1989, Mosaic 1993, VSNL on 15 August 1995.

## gc-comp-06 (e-mail)
- POP3 full form changed to "Post Office Protocol, version 3".
- Tomlinson (1971), Hotmail (1996, Bhatia and Smith) and Gmail (2004) are correct.

## gc-ict-01 (ICT concept)
- No errors found. The UNESCO definition, Kevin Ashton (IoT, 1999), Digital India (1 July 2015, nine pillars) and E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022 (EPR) are correct.

## gc-ict-02 (applications, cloud, cyber security)
- Two-factor authentication: "a password plus an OTP" now reads "for example, a password plus an OTP", because an OTP is only one possible second factor.
- CERT-In (MeitY), IT Act 2000 and helpline 1930 are correct.

## gc-ict-03 (ICT in education)
- DIKSHA "Run by" changed from "NCERT" to "NCERT, for the Ministry of Education". The platform is a Ministry of Education initiative, and NCERT runs it.
- UTSAH was confirmed by web search: UGC launched it on 16 May 2023 to track how institutions carry out NEP 2020.
- SAKSHAM: news reports confirm a UGC SAKSHAM portal for reports on women's safety and gender sensitisation. Its launch year could not be confirmed; the notes give none. The name goes back to the UGC SAKSHAM task-force report of 2013.
- DISE (1995, NIEPA, DPEP, UNICEF support), U-DISE (2012–13), UDISE+ (2018–19, 11-digit code, DoSEL), eGyanKosh (IGNOU, 2005), NDLI (IIT Kharagpur), Shodhganga and e-PG Pathshala (INFLIBNET) were checked from knowledge.

## gc-ict-04 (e-governance)
- Hindi SMART expansion: A = "जवाबदेह" (accountable), R = "संवेदनशील (उत्तरदायी)" (responsive). Before, उत्तरदायी stood for A, which was ambiguous.
- Delhi's e-ration card service (the first in the country, March 2015) was confirmed by web search.
- NIC 1976, NeGP 2006, DBT 1 January 2013, Aadhaar Act 2016, UPI and GeM 2016, e-NAM 2016, e-RUPI 2021 and DPDP Act 2023 are correct.

## Not independently verified online
- The launch year of the UGC SAKSHAM portal (no year is stated in the notes).
- Whether NCERT or the Ministry is the formal "owner" of DIKSHA. The wording now covers both.
