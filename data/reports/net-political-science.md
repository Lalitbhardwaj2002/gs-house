### ps-u1-01 Liberty
- Corrected: Berlin's two questions were given in quotation marks in wording that is not his: "Over what area am I left alone?" / "Who or what controls me?". They now read "How far does government interfere with me?" / "Who governs me?", which is Berlin's own wording in 'Two Concepts of Liberty' (1958). The Hindi was changed to match.
- Added: Quentin Skinner, 'Liberty before Liberalism' (1998), in the point on republican liberty.

### ps-u1-03 Justice

- Hindi: "(डिफरेंस प्रिंसिपल)" added after भेदमूलक सिद्धांत.

### ps-u1-04 Rights
- Corrected (Hindi): "nonsense upon stilts" was translated as "बैसाखियों पर खड़ी बकवास". बैसाखी means crutches, not stilts. It now reads "बाँसों पर चढ़ी बकवास", in the text and in the flash card.

### ps-u1-05 Democracy
- Corrected: "Mosca ('The Ruling Class', 1896)". The 1896 book is 'Elementi di scienza politica'; the English translation 'The Ruling Class' came out in 1939. It now says "'Elements of Political Science', 1896; in English 'The Ruling Class', 1939".
- Corrected: the flash card "Who coined polyarchy? Robert Dahl". Dahl first used the term with Charles Lindblom in 'Politics, Economics and Welfare' (1953). The card now says "Who gave the concept of polyarchy? Robert Dahl (first with Charles Lindblom, 1953)".
- Hindi: "गुटतंत्र का लौह नियम" is now "अल्पतंत्र का लौह नियम", the usual term in Hindi books (3 places).

### ps-u1-06 Power
- Corrected: "mobilisation of bias" was given only under Bachrach and Baratz. The phrase is E. E. Schattschneider's ('The Semisovereign People', 1960), and Bachrach and Baratz borrowed it. This is now stated.
- Hindi: coercion was translated as "दमन" (repression); it is now "दबाव". "गुटतंत्र" is now "अल्पतंत्र".

- Added: the term "night-watchman state" was coined by Ferdinand Lassalle, in mockery (flash card).

### ps-u1-09 Conservatism
- Hindi: "जैविक समाज" is now "आंगिक समाज", the standard term for organic society (2 places). "पितृसत्तात्मक" (patriarchal) is now "पितृवादी" (paternalist) for the One Nation strand.

### ps-u1-10 Socialism
- Corrected: the note said that Marx and Engels gave the name "utopian socialists". The label was in use before them; they made it famous. It now says so.
- Added: 'Dr. Balram Singh v. Union of India' (25 November 2024; 2024 INSC 893). The Supreme Court dismissed the petitions against the words "socialist" and "secular" in the Preamble. Sources: LiveLaw, Verdictum and Bar & Bench reports of 25 November 2024.

### ps-u1-11 Marxism
- Corrected: "withers away" is Engels's phrase ('Anti-Dühring', 1878); this is now stated.
- Corrected: the timeline did not show which works were published much later. It now says: the 'Economic and Philosophic Manuscripts' were written in 1844 and published in 1932; the 'Theses on Feuerbach' and 'The German Ideology' were written in 1845 to 1846 and published later; Lenin's 'Imperialism' was written in 1916; 'The State and Revolution' was written in 1917 and published in 1918.
- Added: Engels, 'The Origin of the Family, Private Property and the State' (1884), in the timeline; Althusser, 'Ideology and Ideological State Apparatuses' (1970), in the table.

### ps-u1-12 Feminism
- Corrected: "'The personal is political' comes from Carol Hanisch's essay (1970)". Hanisch has said that the editors gave the essay its title and that she did not coin the phrase. The note now says the slogan "became famous as the title of" her essay.

### ps-u1-13 Ecologism
- Corrected: Chipko (1973) began in what was then Uttar Pradesh; the area is now in Uttarakhand.
- Added: the year of Lovelock's 'Gaia: A New Look at Life on Earth' (1979). Ramachandra Guha's critique of deep ecology from the Third World ('Radical American Environmentalism and Wilderness Preservation: A Third World Critique', 1989).

### ps-u1-15 Postmodernism
- Corrected (Hindi): "late capitalism" was translated as "उत्तर-पूँजीवाद" (post-capitalism). It is now "उत्तरकालीन पूँजीवाद".
- Corrected: the reference "Gauba (sections on post-modernism)" could not be confirmed. The publisher's description confirms only a "postmodern perspective on power", so the reference now names that.
