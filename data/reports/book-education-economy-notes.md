# Education and Economy notes of the book companion: fact-check of 13 November 2026

Checker Y checked the 11 chapters gc-edu-01 to 03 and gc-eco-01 to 08 in English and Hindi. Changing facts were searched online. `tools/check_book.py` shows no problems for these files after the edits.

## gc-edu-01 Education from British rule to the national policies

## gc-edu-02 The Constitution and the Right to Education
- Article 29 said "Minorities may conserve..." and gave the bar on refusing admission for every institution. It now says "any section of citizens" may conserve its language, script and culture, and that the bar on refusing admission applies to State-run or State-aided institutions (English and Hindi).
- The 2019 rule on holding back said only "fails in Class 5 or Class 8". It now says the child can be held back after failing the exam and then a re-examination within two months (English and Hindi).
- Also checked, correct: Articles 21A, 28, 30, 45, 46, 51A(k), 350A and 351; the 42nd and 86th Amendments; Unni Krishnan 1993; RTE dates (August 2009, 1 April 2010), 25 per cent seats, ratios 30:1 and 35:1, School Management Committee, 200 and 220 working days, Pramati 2014, NCPCR; scheme years from Operation Blackboard to PM SHRI (2022).

## gc-edu-03 Accreditation, institutions of higher education and NEP 2020
- The gross enrolment ratio in higher education said "a little under 30 per cent". The AISHE 2023–24 report (out in July 2026) puts it at 30.0. It now says "about 30 per cent (AISHE 2023–24)", short of the target of 50 (English and Hindi).
- The NIRF heads were not named exactly. They now read: teaching, learning and resources; research and professional practice; graduation outcomes; outreach and inclusivity; perception (English and Hindi).
- Also checked, correct: NAAC 1994 Bengaluru, NBA 1994, NIRF 2015/2016, NTA 2017; the kinds of universities; the university years; NEP 2020 (29 July 2020, Kasturirangan, 5+3+3+4 ages, the targets for 2030 and 2035, 6 per cent of GDP, HECI, 4-year B.Ed. by 2030); SDG 4; UNESCO at Paris.

## gc-eco-02 Poverty, unemployment and welfare schemes
- The new rural employment law had no name. It now names the **Viksit Bharat – Guarantee for Rozgar and Ajeevika Mission (Gramin) Act, 2025** (VB-G RAM G), passed in December 2025. The line says it replaced MGNREGA from 1 July 2026 and promises 125 days. The MGNREGA row now says "(until 2026)" (English and Hindi).
- The 2022–23 multidimensional poverty figure of 11 per cent is an estimate in NITI Aayog's paper, so it now says "an estimated 11 per cent" (English and Hindi).
- Also checked, correct: Alagh, Lakdawala, Tendulkar (21.9 per cent) and Rangarajan; 12 MPI indicators and 29 per cent in 2013–14; CPI by NSO; WPI by the Office of the Economic Adviser; the inflation target of 4 ± 2 per cent, renewed for 2026–31; the scheme years.

## gc-eco-04 Agriculture, industry and infrastructure
- The note said there are **eight** core industries with about 40 per cent weight in the IIP. The new Index of Core Industries (base year 2022–23, July 2026) added **iron ore** as the ninth, and the core group now has about a third of the IIP weight (32.88 per cent). The point, the recall card ("Nine") and the recap line were changed (English and Hindi).
- Also checked, correct: the colour revolutions; CACP and MSP; FCI 1965; NABARD 1982; KCC 1998, Soil Health Card 2015, PMFBY 2016, e-NAM 2016, PM-KISAN 2019; the industrial policies of 1948, 1956 and 1991; MSMED Act 2006; Make in India 2014, Startup India 2016, PLI 2020; Kandla 1965.

## gc-eco-05 Money, inflation and banking
- The reverse repo rate was shown as the active tool. It now adds that since 2022 the Standing Deposit Facility does this job (English and Hindi).
- SLR was "gold and Government securities". It now says cash, gold and Government securities (English and Hindi).
- The priority sector line said all "banks" must lend 40 per cent. It now says commercial banks 40 per cent and Regional Rural Banks 75 per cent (English and Hindi).
- Deposit insurance: still ₹5 lakh, which is correct. The line now names DICGC and adds "since 2020" (English and Hindi).
- Also checked, correct: the one-rupee note and coins (Government) and notes of ₹2 and above (RBI); demonetisation; RBI dates, Osborne Smith and C. D. Deshmukh; MPC of six; the banking timeline; twelve public sector banks (still twelve); Prathama Bank at Moradabad; SIDBI at Lucknow; UPI and NPCI; e-rupee 2022.

## gc-eco-06 Financial markets
- PFRDA had only "2003". It now adds that it became statutory under an Act of 2013 and has its headquarters at New Delhi (English and Hindi).
- Also checked, correct: T-bill tenors; BSE 1875 and Sensex 30; NSE 1992 and Nifty 50; SEBI 1988/1992 at Mumbai; IRDAI 1999 at Hyderabad; NSDL and CDSL; LIC 1956; GIFT City.

## gc-eco-07 Public finance: budget, taxes and the Finance Commission
- The Consolidated Fund said "all receipts go in", which is wrong because Public Account money does not. It now says revenues, loans raised and loan repayments go in, and nothing comes out without a law of Parliament. The recall card was changed to match (English and Hindi).
- The cess and the surcharge were both called "a tax on a tax". Now: a surcharge is a tax on a tax, and a cess is raised for a stated purpose; neither is shared with the States (English and Hindi).
- The GST slabs now name the special 40 per cent rate. "Petrol, diesel and liquor are outside it" is now worded so that liquor for drinking is outside GST and petrol and diesel are not yet taxed under it (constitutionally they are inside GST but not yet notified) (English and Hindi).
- The 16th Finance Commission now adds that it kept the States' share at 41 per cent (report tabled 1 February 2026) (English and Hindi).
- Also checked, correct: Articles 110, 112, 266, 267, 279A and 280; James Wilson 1860; Shanmukham Chetty 1947; railway budget merger 2017; FRBM 2003; 101st Amendment and 1 July 2017; K. C. Neogy; N. K. Singh 41 per cent.

## gc-eco-08 Foreign trade and world economic bodies
- The reserves line left out the reserve position in the IMF. It is now added (English and Hindi).
- The trade-partner line gains "these ranks can change from year to year". For 2025–26 the USA was still the top export market and China the top source of imports (China is now the largest trading partner overall) (English and Hindi).
- Hindi only: the remittance line could be read as "India gets more from its people abroad than from any other country". It now says India is first in the world in receiving money from its people abroad.
- Also checked, correct: devaluations of 1949, 1966 and 1991; market rate since 1993; FEMA 1999; IMF, World Bank, WTO, ADB, NDB and OPEC years and headquarters; IDA; G20 presidency 2023; SAARC at Kathmandu; ASEAN at Jakarta; EXIM Bank 1982.

## Not verified online
- The exact founding years of Kendriya Vidyalaya Sangathan (1963; it was registered as a society in 1965), Navodaya Vidyalaya Samiti (1986) and NIOS (1989, then called NOS) were checked from memory, not online. They were left as written.
- The new core-industry weight (32.88 per cent of IIP) comes from coaching and news summaries of the official release, not from the release itself. The text says only "about a third".
