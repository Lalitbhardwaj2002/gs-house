# geo-climate: checker report

The draft had 55 questions, and all **55 were kept**: 30 were corrected and none deleted. The ids are unchanged (geo-climate-001 to -055, with no gaps). I solved every question blind and every answer key was confirmed. Answers are spread A–D = 14/14/14/13. Levels: TGT 19, PGT 21, Higher 15.

## References checked
- **Fatman**: I opened printed pp. 20–23 (images p028–p031) and 47–49 (images p056–p058), and confirmed the printed page numbers (image = printed + 8 for pp. 20–23, + 9 for pp. 47–49). Every Fatman page cited in the section was seen. Three refs were removed because their pages do not carry the fact (Q20 p. 20, Q30 p. 48, Q52 p. 48).
- **Fatman errors the questions avoid**: The local-winds table on p. 22 is scrambled. It puts the Sirocco in Siberia/Canada, the Khamsin in the Gulf of Guinea and the Harmattan in Egypt, and calls the Föhn cold. None of these is used. Do not cite p. 22 for local winds.
- **NCERT Class 9 Ch. 4 (iess104, 2023 rationalised edition)**: I read the full text. It confirms the following:
  - "mausim" (Arabic) and the six climatic controls
  - the cold season (mid-November to February), mahawat, and western disturbances from the Mediterranean
  - kaal baisakhi, mango showers, the monsoon-trough "break" mechanism, October heat, the Andaman Sea cyclones (Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri deltas), Mawsynram in the southern Khasi ranges, and the east-to-west decrease of rainfall in the Ganga valley
  - Drass at −45°C and the Thar at 50°C by day and near 15°C at night
  - higher variability of rainfall in low-rainfall areas, and the station table

  The rationalised edition **no longer has** the jet-stream, "Indian Monsoon" factor, onset-date and El Niño sections. Where the draft cited Class 9 for those facts (Q6, Q14, Q17), I switched the ref to NCERT Class 11 (which has them) or to a Down To Earth / IMD source.
- **NCERT Class 11 India: Physical Environment Ch. 4 (rationalised)**: I read the text. It confirms the following:
  - the ITCZ at 20°–25°N in July, the cross-equatorial flow at 40°–60°E, and the easterly jet at 15°N ("burst")
  - Kerala onset by 1 June, Mumbai and Kolkata on 10–13 June
  - "break" defined as one or more weeks, and west-coast breaks caused by coast-parallel winds
  - the four seasons; Caspian–Turkmenistan cold waves; Delhi's 53 mm winter rain; 25–50 mm in Assam and Arunachal; 38°C / 38–43°C / 48°C in the hot season
  - mango showers, blossom showers, Kalbaisakhi, **Bardoisila** (Assam) and loo blowing "well into midnight"
  - the Arakan hills; the Arabian Sea branches (250–400 cm, Narmada–Tapi, Saurashtra–Aravalli scanty); the Tamil Nadu coast dry because it lies parallel to the Bay branch and in the rain shadow
  - October–November as the rainiest months on the Coromandel coast; the retreat sequence (first week of September, then October, early November, mid-December)
  - El Niño ("Child Christ", every 3–7 years, used for long-range forecasts) and Tahiti–Port Darwin (12°30'S, 131°E)
  - Mawsynram and Cherrapunji over 1,080 cm; Jaisalmer under 9 cm; Tura in one day equal to 10 years at Jaisalmer; Churu 50°C and Tawang 19°C; Kerala and Andaman diurnal range 7–8°C; 125 cm average rainfall

  Koeppen and the coefficient of variation are not in the rationalised edition, so those refs stay marked "pre-2023 edition".
- **IMD and other sources**:
  - Down To Earth: the 2020 normals (onset from 1961–2019 data, withdrawal from 1971–2019 data, the old normals from 149 stations for 1901–1940, withdrawal from north-west India on 17 September instead of 1 September).
  - News On AIR / IMD DG: the whole country is covered by the normal date of 8 July. The complete-withdrawal normal is 15 October.
  - The Wire: the LPA of 868.6 mm (87 cm) for 1971–2020, an annual normal of 1,160.1 mm, and the monsoon's 74.9% share.
  - WMO list: Montha and Sitrang are Thailand's names, there are 13 panel members, and names are not reused.
  - Tribune: Montha's landfall on 28 October 2025, and its name means "fragrant flower" in Thai.
  - NOAA CPC: the definition of a positive IOD. An IMD DG quote via Scroll: a positive IOD is good for Indian rainfall.
  - Patna Women's College: Halley (1686) and Flohn (1951).
  - Koteswaram (1958, Tellus) and Walker: Southern Oscillation.
