# Indian History notes (chapters 1 to 11) of the book companion: fact-check of 13 November 2026

Files checked: data/book/gc-hist-01.txt to gc-hist-11.txt, English and Hindi parts. The book checker (tools/check_book.py) shows no problems for these files after the edits.

## gc-hist-01 Prehistory and the Indus Valley Civilisation
No errors found. Checked the stone-age sites, Robert Bruce Foote (1863), the excavators of Harappa and Mohenjo-daro (1921, 1922), the site table (Lothal, Kalibangan, Dholavira 2021, Rakhigarhi, Chanhudaro, Alamgirpur), Mehrgarh, the weights and the dates 2600 to 1900 BCE.

## gc-hist-02 The Vedic age
- Hindi only: "दस राजाओं का युद्ध" now also gives the usual exam name "(दाशराज्ञ युद्ध)".
Otherwise no errors found. Checked the two periods, rivers, Parushni, 1,028 hymns and ten mandalas, Gayatri mantra (third mandala), Purusha Sukta, Mundaka Upanishad, Vedangas and the founders of the six schools.

## gc-hist-03 Buddhism and Jainism
- The dates of the Buddha (563 and 483 BCE) and of Mahavira (540 and 468 BCE) were given as exact. They are the textbook dates but scholars differ, so they now read "about 563 BCE" and so on, in both languages.
Otherwise checked: places and symbols of the Buddha's life, the four councils, Tirthankaras, five vows, Jain councils, Shravanabelagola.

## gc-hist-04 Mahajanapadas, Magadha and the Maurya Empire
- The opening point said "In the sixth century BCE Buddhist texts list sixteen great states", which reads as if the texts were written then. It now says that Buddhist texts such as the Anguttara Nikaya list sixteen great states of the sixth century BCE (both languages).
- English only: the trick box was called "three C's of Chandragupta", but its three names are Chanakya, Seleucus and the Indica. It is now "three names around Chandragupta", matching the Hindi.
Otherwise checked: capitals, Magadha timeline, Alexander and Porus, Megasthenes, Kalinga war (261 BCE), Brahmi and Prinsep (1837), the edicts, provinces and Brihadratha (185 BCE).

## gc-hist-05 After the Mauryas, the Sangam age and Roman trade
- Menander's row said he was the "first to issue gold coins in India". Exam books give this to the Indo-Greeks as a people, not to Menander. It now says "The Indo-Greeks were the first to issue gold coins in India".
- "Three Sangams ... under the Pandya kings at Madurai" was wrong: by tradition only the third met at Madurai. It now says "the last of them at Madurai".
Otherwise checked: Shungas, Kanvas, Satavahanas, Kharavela, Rudradaman, Gondophernes, Kanishka and 78 CE, art schools, Karikala, ports, Hippalus, Pliny and the Periplus.

## gc-hist-06 The Gupta Empire, Harsha and the centres of learning
- The science table listed "Sushruta and Dhanvantari" under the Gupta age. Sushruta belongs to an earlier time. The row now gives only Dhanvantari, the physician who was one of the nine gems of Chandragupta II.
- Brahmagupta (born 598) lived after the Guptas; his row now says "seventh century, just after the Guptas".
- "Ajanta caves were cut under the Vakatakas" now says "the later Ajanta caves", since the first caves are much older.
Otherwise checked: Gupta reigns, Gupta era 319-20, Harishena, Fa-Hien, Nalanda, Kalidasa and other authors, Deogarh and Bhitargaon, Harsha (606 to 647), Pulakeshin II, Banabhatta and the centres of learning.

## gc-hist-07 South India and the early medieval kingdoms
- The Pallava row said Narasimhavarman I built the rathas and the Shore Temple. The Shore Temple was built by Narasimhavarman II (Rajasimha). It now says Narasimhavarman I cut the rathas and Narasimhavarman II built the Shore Temple.
- English only: the Uttaramerur sentence read as if the committees themselves were written on palm leaves. It now says that names were written on palm leaves and drawn from a pot (the Hindi already said this).
Otherwise checked: capitals, Kailasa temple (Krishna I), Rajaraja I and Rajendra I, ur, sabha and nagaram, the struggle for Kannauj, Rajput houses, 712, 1025, Tarain 1191 and 1192, Chandawar 1194, Al-Biruni and Firdausi.

## gc-hist-08 The Delhi Sultanate, Vijayanagara and the Bahmani kingdom
- Razia was called "the only woman to rule from Delhi", which is too broad. It now says "the only woman Sultan of Delhi".
- The true arch and dome were illustrated with the Qutub Minar, which is a tower and not an example. It now gives Balban's tomb (first true arch) and the Alai Darwaza (first true dome), with the Lodi tombs.
Otherwise checked: dynasty years, the great Sultans, Timur 1398, Amir Khusrau, the departments, taxes, Vijayanagara (1336, four dynasties, Krishnadevaraya, travellers, Talikota 1565) and the Bahmani kingdom.

## gc-hist-09 The Mughal Empire
No errors found. Checked the reigns, battles (1526, 1527, 1539, 1540, 1556, 1576, 1739), Sher Shah, mansabdari, zabti, Ibadat Khana 1575, Din-i-Ilahi 1582, the nine gems, Tulsidas and the Sikh Gurus.

## gc-hist-10 The Marathas
No errors found. Checked Shivaji's timeline (1630 to 1680), the Ashtapradhan, chauth and sardeshmukhi, Sambhaji 1689, the Peshwas and their years, Panipat (14 January 1761), the confederacy seats and the three Anglo-Maratha wars.

## gc-hist-11 The Europeans and the growth of British rule
No errors found. Checked the company dates and factories, Colachel 1741, Wandiwash 1760, Plassey, Buxar, Allahabad, dual government, Subsidiary Alliance, Doctrine of Lapse, the three revenue systems and the Governors-General and Viceroys table.

## Not verified online
No web search was used; all checks were made against the standard textbook and exam-book facts. These points follow the usual exam answer although scholars differ, and were left as they are:
- Kanishka as the starter of the Shaka era of 78 CE (gc-hist-05).
- Amaravati sculpture "white marble" (it is really a pale limestone; exam books say marble) (gc-hist-05).
- Ashoka's reign "about 268 to 232 BCE" (some books give 273 BCE as his accession) (gc-hist-04).
- Odantapuri founded by Gopala (some books say Dharmapala) (gc-hist-06).
- Warren Hastings as Governor-General in 1773 (he took office in 1774) (gc-hist-11).
