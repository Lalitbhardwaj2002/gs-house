# Environment and Health notes of the book companion: fact-check of 13 November 2026

## gc-env-01 Environment and ecosystem
- Nitrogen cycle row (both languages): it said "other bacteria turn it into nitrates and return it to the air", which mixes up two groups of bacteria. It now says nitrifying bacteria make nitrates and denitrifying bacteria return it to the air.
- Also checked: Haeckel 1866, Tansley 1935, Lindeman 1942, the pyramids, biomes and succession.

## gc-env-03 Protected areas
- Hindi only: the tiger reserve was called "बाघ अभयारण्य", which a learner can mix up with a wildlife sanctuary (अभयारण्य). It now says "बाघ आरक्षित क्षेत्र (टाइगर रिजर्व)".
- Also checked: Wild Life Act 1972, NBWL under the PM, 42nd Amendment, 18 biosphere reserves (13 in the UNESCO network), Nilgiri 1986, Rann of Kachchh the largest, Corbett 1936, Hemis the largest park, Madhya Pradesh most parks, Project Tiger 1973, crocodile project 1975, Project Elephant 1992, NTCA 2006, Snow Leopard 2009, Project Dolphin 2020, Project Cheetah 2022, tiger estimate 3,682 (2022; the 2026 count is not yet published), Madhya Pradesh most tigers, the park table.

## gc-env-04 Wetlands, mangroves and forests
- Ramsar sites (both languages): it said "more than 90", with no counts. It now says India has 103 Ramsar sites (October 2026, after Sukhna Wetland and Sauj Jheel were added), Tamil Nadu 20, Uttar Pradesh 14.
- Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980 (both languages): added that it was renamed Van (Sanrakshan Evam Samvardhan) Adhiniyam in 2023.
- Also checked: Ramsar 2 February 1971, India joined 1982, Chilika and Keoladeo the first sites (1981), Sundarban Wetland the largest, Keoladeo and Loktak on the Montreux Record, the Uttar Pradesh sites, mangrove cover (West Bengal, then Gujarat), Gahirmatha, ISFR 2023 (25.17 per cent; Madhya Pradesh the largest forest cover), Forest Policy 1988, Khejarli 1730 (Amrita Devi and 362 others, 363 in all), Van Mahotsav 1950, Chipko 1973, Appiko 1983, Silent Valley 1984.

## gc-env-07 Climate change and ozone
